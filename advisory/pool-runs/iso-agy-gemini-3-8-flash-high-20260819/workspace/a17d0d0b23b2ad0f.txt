## Revision record {#el:revision}

`implementation-plan-review` refused the prior candidate with verdict
`needs_revision` and exactly one finding, at severity major, on review ledger
`85859`. The review's summary records that the revision "adds a concrete
telemetry store, Driver read path, and delivered-path guards, but its live
cold-start specification is internally contradictory and cannot satisfy the
evidence-cold-degrades clause or its registered evaluator node as written" — so
the refusal is not about the live evidence path the prior revision added. It is
about what that path is required to *record and emit* when it finds no evidence.

**RPL-008 — the delivered no-store path had two incompatible variants and
bypassed the prior rung.** *Element anchors:*
[W9](#el:w9-evidence-context), [W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam),
[W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring), [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance),
[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades), and work-graph packets `p18-guard-dominance-demotes`
and `p20-guard-degrades-to-declared-order`. *Falsified clause:*
`evidence-cold-degrades`, and with it the `guard-degrades-to-declared-order`
evaluator node. Two distinct defects, both in the delivered-path statements the
prior revision added:

- **The variant conflation.** [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).7 and
  [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).9 define `disabled` for exactly one input — an
  operative validated policy that declares no `telemetry` block — and put a store
  that returns nothing on the other axis entirely, as an `excluded` slot inside
  an `enabled` context. [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) requires the delivered
  document to declare an enabled `telemetry` block. Yet
  [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance)'s *Nothing else moves* bullet said removing that
  document's store "is how the evidence context becomes `disabled` on this path",
  and [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s delivered-path clause and `p20`'s purpose
  required the same no-store run to record `context_variant: disabled`. The two
  cannot both be implemented. Following W9/W14 makes the registered `p20` guard
  fail on the delivered tree; satisfying W18/W20 records missing evidence as a
  policy that declares none, which is a false statement about the operative
  document in every trace the path produces.
- **The skipped rung.** [W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).8 states the ladder the
  accepted clause states: losing Tier 1 falls **first** to the pinned
  projection's order over the applicable priors, and only where no applicable
  prior exists is the emitted order `base_order` byte-for-byte.
  [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring)'s verification, [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s
  delivered-path clause and `p20`'s purpose each asserted the `base_order`
  equality for a no-store wake **unconditionally**, and
  [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path)'s verification said the four degraded read
  states produce "the ordinary cold-start order" without saying which rung that
  is. A guard written to those statements checks the second rung while asserting
  it is the whole floor.

**The finding is accepted in full, and it names the cause rather than a
symptom.** The first defect is a contradiction inside this plan and needs no
argument beyond exhibiting it. The second is worse than an imprecision, and the
staged tree is why: `backends/observations/artificial-analysis.json` is an
accepted model-scoped observation snapshot over the fleet's models, so an
applicable prior is not a hypothetical in this repository — it is the ordinary
case. A delivered-path guard asserting `base_order` unconditionally would either
fail against a correct implementation or, worse, pass against one that dropped
the prior rung, which is exactly the "silently retire the accepted @2 behavior"
the design forbids. `evidence-cold-degrades` names two rungs and says harness
siblings are not silently pooled; a plan that names one rung does not state it.

**It is the same shape as [RPL-007](#el:revision-history), one level of
description down.** RPL-007 was a mechanism nothing called; RPL-008 is a called
mechanism the plan described wrongly in the one place a reader would check what
calling it does. Both are invisible to `go build`, `go vet` and the unit suites,
and both are visible only where the property is stated against the delivered
path. The disposition is correspondingly narrow: no seam moves, no packet is
added, and every correction lands in a statement about what the delivered path
records and emits.

### What this revision changes {#el:revision-additions}

Five things, and nothing else.

1. **`disabled` is reserved, and the no-store state is named exactly.**
   [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).8 makes the two axes independent as a checkable
   property of the assembly: an operative policy declaring a `telemetry` block
   yields an `enabled` context **whatever the store returned**, and a store that
   returns no candidate bytes yields an `excluded` slot whose reason list is
   exactly `[not_available]`, with no candidate digest and no observed envelope
   because neither existed. `disabled` has exactly one producer — a validated
   policy with no `telemetry` block — and no store state is one.
   [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path).3 and [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).9 are
   restated to agree, and [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance) and
   [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) now assert the corrected states.
2. **Both cold-start rungs are exercised on the delivered path.**
   [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s delivered-path clause becomes three driven
   wakes — no store, an excluded store, and the telemetry-block-removed mutation
   — each asserted on **both** rungs: the emitted order equals byte-for-byte what
   the pinned projection emits for that wake's applicable prior set, and equals
   `base_order` byte-for-byte exactly in the sub-case where that set is empty,
   with the emptiness established rather than assumed.
   [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring)'s verification and
   [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path)'s verification are restated the same way.
3. **The live prior source is named.** [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).12 states
   where a running wake's applicable prior set comes from — the accepted
   model-scoped observation snapshots under `backends/observations/`, read
   through the pinned projection record's prior schema — and that `priors: []` is
   the decided empty state when none applies rather than an omission.
   [EN13](#el:en13-live-prior-source) records the location and its alternatives,
   and [W21](#el:w21-delivery).8 documents both rungs and the source of each, so
   implementation does not outrun explanation (D0001.C13). Without this, the
   first rung has no source in this repository and the second defect is
   unfixable rather than merely unstated.
4. **The fixture corpus gains the data the corrected clauses range over.**
   [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8 adds, for the delivered path, an applicable
   model-scoped prior set and a set under which no prior applies, and the
   telemetry-block-removed policy mutation as a driven-wake input rather than
   only as a pure-seam one.
5. **One finding against the design and two risks.**
   [F15](#el:f15-live-prior-source-unnamed) records that the design fixes the
   prior rung's semantics and names no source a live wake draws its priors from —
   the same kind of gap as [F14](#el:f14-live-path-unnamed) and closed the same
   way. The two risks name the two failure modes RPL-008 found: an order that is
   right for the wrong reason, because the no-prior floor and the prior-informed
   order coincide whenever the fixture happens to carry no applicable prior; and
   a conflated variant, which is invisible to every mechanical check that does
   not read a trace.

### The disposition, clause by clause {#el:revision-disposition}

The review proposed a disposition in two halves. Both are taken without
modification.

**Half one — "revise the W14 no-store verification, W18 live evidence-off
comparison, W20 delivered-path clause, and p18/p20 purposes so an enabled policy
with no store records an enabled context whose snapshot slot is excluded with
`not_available`; reserve `disabled` for a policy with no telemetry block."**
Taken. All four sites are rewritten to that reading, and two more the review did
not name are rewritten with them because they carry the same defect:
[W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path).3, which described the four degraded read
states without saying what variant they produce, and
[W9](#el:w9-evidence-context), which stated the rule correctly and did not make
it checkable. The exclusion reason is the design's own: C27(b) fixes
`[not_available]` as the exact list "with no candidate bytes", and it is the
first member of the closed ordered set, so nothing is minted and no reason is
chosen where the design leaves a choice.

**Half two — "make the delivered-path guard exercise both cold-start rungs
explicitly: with an applicable prior it must equal the pinned projection's
order, and only with no applicable prior may it equal `base_order`
byte-for-byte."** Taken. [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s delivered-path clause
states the two rungs as two assertions over each of its three driven wakes, and
[W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8 supplies the two prior sets that make the
sub-case reachable and distinguishable. The pure-seam clause of
[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) already carried both rungs and its live per-pass
sweep is now explicitly the empty-prior sub-case, which is what makes its
byte-for-byte equality with today's `Bind` order a true statement rather than an
accident.

**One consequence is stated rather than absorbed.** Naming the live prior source
makes the prior rung operative on the delivered path, so the order a wake emits
need **not** equal today's `Bind` order wherever an applicable prior exists. That
is not a change this plan chooses: it is the accepted @2 behaviour the pinned
predicate requires cold start to fall back to first, and the design says in as
many words that treating telemetry absence as direct Tier 3 "would silently
retire the accepted @2 behavior". Every equality with today's order in this plan
is therefore scoped to the empty-prior sub-case, and none of them was weakened:
`base_of`'s per-pass equality ([W5](#el:w5-backend-context).4) is about
`base_order` and not about the emitted order, and the placement-baseline suite
calls the scheduler seam with a policy-derived preference list, upstream of any
of this.

**No sixth check identity is minted, for the reasons the prior revision
recorded.** The evaluator's five check ids are exactly the members of
`placement-outcome-guards` in `policy/checks/repository.json` at
`registry_version: 15`; D0005.C9 requires that set to be resolved to exact
identities and D0005.C6 makes the evaluator part of what a gate result pins, so
adding a member would edit an environment element this run consumes by pin. Both
of RPL-008's corrections land inside `guard-degrades-to-declared-order` and
`guard-dominance-demotes`, which are the nodes covering the clauses it falsifies,
and `policy/checks/repository.json` stays untouched by every packet.

## What this plan is and is not {#el:scope}

This plan states **how** the design's ten elements are encoded in the staged
tree: which existing files carry which seam, which files are new, in what
semantic order the work applies, and how each boundary is verified. It
introduces no architectural decision. Where a choice was genuinely open — a
package, a file name, a document location, an application order — it is recorded
as an [encoding decision](#el:encoding) with its reason, and each such choice is
one the design leaves to L3 by construction (the design's own
`What this design does not carry` section places staging, decomposition and
dependency ordering at L3/L4). [EN10](#el:en10-document-before-surface) is the
one that is an *ordering* rather than a placement, and it is the most clearly
delegated of them: the design says what a validated policy yields and says
nothing about when the repository's own document acquires one.

Three things are deliberately **absent**:

- **No relitigation of the design.** Where this plan disagrees with a seam
  description, it says so as a finding and then implements the design as
  written under the most conservative reading. It never substitutes a different
  semantics. **Two** places go further than a design's *stated check*, both of
  the same kind — the design fixes a semantics and states a checkable form
  weaker than it — and both are lowerings rather than amendments because every
  condition the design states remains true of everything this plan admits:
  - [F9](#el:f9-completion-mass)'s **completion-mass equality**. The design
    states its body domains as a necessary-condition list ("well-formed **only
    if** …") and its own fold semantics entail a condition the list omits.
    Adding it refuses only bodies the design's semantics already exclude.
  - [F10](#el:f10-influence-declaration)'s **`influence` domain**. The design
    fixes influence as prefer-only outright and its policy shape does not
    enumerate the member, while this repository's operative document declares
    it. Giving it a domain of exactly the one value the design admits refuses
    only documents declaring an influence the accepted predicate does not have,
    adds no refusal condition to the closed set, and — being optional — refuses
    no document the design's stated list admits. [EN8](#el:en8-influence-declaration)
    records the reading and its two rejected alternatives.

  Nothing else in this plan strengthens a declared domain, and neither of these
  touches a seam shape, a member set or a digest. The matched-rule selector of
  [F11](#el:f11-rule-selector) is **not** a third such case and is not a
  strengthening at all: the design states twice that it does not decide how a
  repository declares policy positions, so choosing that this repository
  declares them per placement rule is a lowering into a space the design left
  open, and it refuses no document, adds no refusal condition and moves no
  digest.
- **No new checks minted in packet gates, and no sixth identity minted at
  all.** The evaluator's five check identities and the
  `placement-outcome-guards` set are **already registered** in
  `policy/checks/repository.json` at `registry_version: 15`; what is missing is
  the five entrypoints those registered argv lines name. Writing them is
  delivery work, so the packets that write them gate on `code` and
  `go test ./...`, and only the final delivery packet names
  `placement-outcome-guards` and `stalls-guards`. This revision keeps the
  registry itself untouched: the end-to-end assertions RPL-007 asks for are
  clauses of the five guards already registered, for the reasons
  [The disposition, clause by clause](#el:revision-disposition) states, so no
  packet edits `policy/checks/repository.json` and the set the pinned evaluator
  names still resolves to exactly its five nodes.
- **No claim that the merit observation is build work.** [W21](#el:w21-delivery)
  authors the derivation-basis Product Artifact that the `observation` pass
  admits as an Exogenous Change Record; running that pass is a separate pass run
  and is recorded in [Deferrals](#el:deferrals).

## Findings against the design {#el:findings}

F1–F8, F11 and F12 are each a definite description in the design or in the
staged tree that does not denote, or denotes more than one thing. F9 and F10 are
the other kind of gap a lowering can find: a description that denotes, but whose
*checkable* form is weaker than the semantics it is stated for, so a conforming
implementation can satisfy every stated condition and still not do what the
design says. F9 is that gap in the snapshot body; F10 is that gap in the
operative policy document. F13 is a third kind and the only one of its kind
here: a fact about the staged tree that no description in the design is wrong
about, but that a lowering which edits the operative policy document has to know
before it edits it. F14 and F15 are a fourth kind, the one
[RPL-007](#el:revision-history) found and [RPL-008](#el:revision) found again one
input over: not a description that fails to denote or is weakly checkable, but a
*seam the design never names at all*, whose absence lets a delivery satisfy every
element the design states and do nothing. F14 is that gap in where a live
placement gets its snapshot; F15 is that gap in where it gets its priors. None is
a reason to withhold work; each names the packet that closes it.

### F1 — The admitted objective-projection record does not exist {#el:f1-projection-record}

[E9](#el:w1-projection-record) requires scheduler policy to declare
`placement_objectives.projection: { id, pin }` resolving to an **admitted
projection record** that publishes a closed objective vocabulary and bears
P-A–P-D. In the staged tree the @2 projection exists only as *code* —
`internal/derived/backend.go`, whose `Objective` constants are the de-facto
vocabulary and whose `ProjectBackends` is the de-facto `prefer` — and there is
no record, no content address, and no `projection` key anywhere in
`policy/scheduler.yaml` or `schema/scheduler-policy.schema.json`. The
description denotes nothing. [W1](#el:w1-projection-record) creates the record
and binds the existing implementation to it; this is the design's own
requirement, not an extension of it.

### F2 — Backend declarations do not declare four of the five carried per-candidate values {#el:f2-backend-positions}

C32 requires the backend context in force at `bind_marker` to declare, for each
`backend_id`, the tuple coordinates `(harness, model, effort)` and the two
ordering positions `declared_policy_position` and `declared_rank`. In the staged
tree a backend declaration (`backends/*/backend.yaml`) carries `runtime_id`
(the harness) and `scheduler_hints: [rank=N]` (the rank) — and the model and the
effort exist **only inside the adapter command argv**, as pinned CLI flags, with
no declared position of their own. `declared_policy_position` has no
representation at all in a backend declaration.

So exactly one of the five (`declared_rank`) denotes today, one is recoverable
by convention (`runtime_id`), and three do not denote. Without them the
effort-sibling grouping of E5 — which is the whole of "unearned effort demotes"
— is not computable from declared data, and conjunct 7 of `coherent` has nothing
to equate against. [W5](#el:w5-backend-context) closes this by declaring the
coordinates explicitly in `backends/`, which is also the only place D0001's
Consequences permit a model name to appear.

### F3 — `declared_policy_position` denotes ambiguously {#el:f3-policy-position}

The design places `declared_policy_position` inside the **backend context**, but
the only datum in the staged tree with that meaning is policy-side: the index of
a backend id in the matched `placement_rules[].prefer.backends` list, which
`internal/scheduler/scheduler.go` consumes today via `preferenceRank` and
`ReadyRun.BackendPreference`. Read as a *new per-backend declared field* it
would duplicate policy in every declaration and drift from it; read as *the
preference index* it is not a fact of the declaration alone.

This plan takes the conservative reading — see
[EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition) — because it is the only one that
keeps `base_of(eligible)` byte-for-byte equal to the order `Bind` already
produces, which is what the predicate's cold-start floor requires. The reading
is recorded in `outputs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

That reading has a consequence this finding does not state and the prior
candidate did not follow through on: the preference index is an index into
**some one** list, and the operative document holds several.
[F11](#el:f11-rule-selector) is that second half.

`policy/checks/repository.json` registers the `placement-outcome-guards` set

### F5 — The live scheduler policy's `telemetry` block is present and incomplete {#el:f5-incomplete-telemetry}

`policy/scheduler.yaml` declares `telemetry: { influence: none, min_samples: 20 }`.
Under E2 refusal condition 5 — "a `telemetry` block is present but incomplete —

This finding is about the members that are **missing**. The member that is
**present** — `influence: none` — is a separate gap with a separate disposition,

### F6 — "the pinned snapshot" denotes two different objects {#el:f6-snapshot-ambiguity}

`internal/scheduler/scheduler.go` already defines `Snapshot` as a one-field

### F8 — Two sibling guard sets remain unbacked and are out of scope {#el:f8-sibling-guard-sets}

`placement-guards` (the @2 set) names three entrypoints of which this delivery

### F9 — The published completion mass is bounded but never closed {#el:f9-completion-mass}

The design's fold semantics say that an outcome sample "counts in `samples`, in
the outcome rates, and in the adverse-completion rate", and that
`adverse_completion` is "the share of outcome samples terminating as error,
abandonment or partial, over `samples`". Read together, those fix a partition:
every sample has one termination, that termination has one category, and the
four published categories therefore exhaust `samples`.

The design's **checkable** statement of the same thing does not close it. The
declared snapshot domain and the body well-formedness list bound the four rates
at "exact rationals in `[0, 1]` summing to **at most** `1`" and require only
that `adverse_completion` equal the published `partial + error + abandonment`;
the sampled/unsampled partition is stated as exhaustive by construction, but the
partition *inside* the sampled side is not. The design's own guard specification
matches: it exercises `samples + unsampled` at every scope, the unsampled-only
scope, the two structural conditions and the cause-outside-the-enumeration case
— every one of them about the outer partition — and states no fixture over the
completion mass.

The consequence is exactly the one the review named. A cell may publish `N`
samples whose four categories account for fewer than `N`; the missing mass is
invisible in the body, invisible in the digest — because it was never folded —
and invisible to derivation agreement, because a rebuild at the same envelope
inputs omits it identically. If any of the missing mass was adverse,
`adverse_completion` is understated and the demotion `unearned-effort-demotes`
requires does not fire, with every declared check accepting.

The staged tree makes the hole concrete rather than theoretical:
`schema/ledger-records/pass_run_closed.schema.json` declares eight recorded
closure outcomes (`submitted`, `submitted_partial`, `submitted_late`,
`abandoned`, `canceled`, `error`, `superseded_by_redispatch`, `completed`), and
the design's rate vocabulary has four members. Some of those eight fall on the
unsampled side under the design's own single predicate, but nothing obliged the
remainder to map onto the four, and `submitted_late` in particular has no
category anyone had to choose.

This plan closes it inside the design rather than against it. The design states
its body domains as conditions a body is well-formed **only if** it satisfies —
necessary conditions, not a closed sufficient set — so the equality is an
additional necessary condition that the design's own fold semantics entail, and
every condition the design does state remains true of every body this plan
admits. [W6](#el:w6-snapshot-shape).5 states the equality,
[W7](#el:w7-telemetry-fold).4 makes the classification total under
[EN7](#el:en7-completion-category), and [W17](#el:w17-guard-fold) and
[W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance) give it teeth. The reading is recorded in
`outputs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

### F10 — The operative policy declares an influence the design's semantics do not have {#el:f10-influence-declaration}

[F5](#el:f5-incomplete-telemetry) is about the seven declared telemetry members
that are absent from `policy/scheduler.yaml`. This finding is about the one that
is present. The live document declares

```yaml
telemetry:
  influence: none                # v1: none; v3: prefer_only — never unlocks forbidden placements
  min_samples: 20
```

and three facts about that line hold together in the staged tree. It is **not
read**: no file under `internal/`, `cmd/` or `tools/` mentions `influence` at
all. It is **not validated**: `schema/scheduler-policy.schema.json` is open at
every level (`additionalProperties: true`) and declares no `telemetry` block, so
nothing constrains the value. And it is **not in the design's shape**: the
design's *Scheduler policy shape* interface enumerates `distance_metric`,
`min_samples`, `freshness_bound`, `lookback_window` and the four `materiality`
margins, and its *Declared value domains* table gives a domain to each of those
and to `projection.id`/`projection.pin` — `influence` appears in neither.

The design's own switch is the block's presence: `thresholds_of` yields the
`enabled` variant exactly when the policy declares a telemetry block, and an
absent block is the fully-specified `disabled` state. So on the design's reading
the completed block of [F5](#el:f5-incomplete-telemetry) turns tuple evidence
on, and `influence: none` is inert text.

**Inert text is exactly the problem.** The line reads, to any consumer and to
any later editor, as the master switch for the capability this delivery exists
to add — its own comment says so. A delivery that completes the block and leaves
the line at `none` ships an operative document that says telemetry is off while
the code has it on. Two failure modes follow, and each is real:

- **An implementation honours it.** Nothing in the prior plan forbade a
  validator or a `thresholds_of` that reads `influence` and returns `disabled`
  on `none`. Every guard would still accept, because the guards range over
  fixture policies; the live repository would simply never demote. That is the
  state RPL-003 named.
- **Nothing honours it and the document lies.** The block is complete, tuple
  evidence is live, higher-effort tuples demote — and the operative declaration
  says `none`. The next editor reading the file to answer "is this on?" gets the
  wrong answer from the only line that claims to answer it.

The target predicate's "policy that looks operative is never silently ignored"
is stated for the first direction. The second is its mirror, and the same rule
disposes of both: a declared value that decides nothing is a value that must not
be declarable. This plan closes it inside the design rather than against it. The
design fixes influence as prefer-only outright — the `telemetry-is-prefer-only`
clause and D0008.C9 — so the value has exactly one admissible reading, and
giving it that domain adds no choice that was open. [EN8](#el:en8-influence-declaration)
states the reading, [W3](#el:w3-policy-validator).10 makes `none` refuse the
load under the refusal condition that already exists,
[W4](#el:w4-policy-document) makes the operative document declare `prefer_only`
and asserts the live `enabled` resolution, and [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance)
gives the whole thing teeth against the delivered document rather than a fixture
one. The reading is recorded in `outputs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

### F11 — "the matched preference list" does not denote without a selector {#el:f11-rule-selector}

[F3](#el:f3-policy-position) settled *which kind of datum*
`declared_policy_position` is: the index of a backend id in the matched
`placement_rules[].prefer.backends` list. This finding is about the word
*matched*. The design places the two ordering positions in the **backend
context**, "for each declared `backend_id`", and says the context in force at a
ledger position is a total function of the ledger up to it. Under the F3
reading, in this repository, it is not — not from the ledger position alone.

The staged tree makes the multiplicity concrete, and in two strengths.

**It is live.** `policy/scheduler.yaml` declares seven `placement_rules`, five
carrying a `prefer.backends` list, and nine backend ids appear in more than one:

| Backend id | Positions it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `claude-harm-opus-4-8-high` | 7 under `build-single-agent-coding`, 0 under `review-timely-first` |
| `claude-opus-5-high` | 2 under `build-single-agent-coding`, 2 under `proposal-constrained-effort`, 1 under `design-needs-quality` |
| `claude-harm-opus-5-high` | 3, 3 and 2 under those same three rules |
| `claude-sonnet-5-high` | 4 under `build-single-agent-coding`, 0 under `proposal-constrained-effort` |
| `claude-harm-sonnet-5-high` | 5 and 1 under the same two |
| `codex-sol-max` | 30 under `build-single-agent-coding`, 2 under `review-timely-first` |
| `codex-luna-medium` | 17 and 4 |
| `codex-luna-max` | 32 under `build-single-agent-coding`, 3 under `design-needs-quality` |
| `codex-terra-max` | 31 and 4 |

**It is not recoverable from anything else.** Two rules do not merely shift a
backend's index; they **invert a pair**. `claude-harm-opus-5-high` precedes
`claude-sonnet-5-high` under `build-single-agent-coding` (indices 3 and 4) and
follows it under `proposal-constrained-effort` (indices 3 and 0). Both
declarations support both passes, and their declared
`scheduler_hints: [rank=…]` values — 23 and 29 — order them the *first* way. So
under `proposal-generation` the declared floor disagrees with the rank order,
and no context that lacks a selector can produce it. A single-valued position
per backend is not an approximation here; it is a different order for at least
one cataloged pass.

The prior [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).3 composed the context from "the
ledger-anchored declarations and the operative validated policy" at a position,
with no Lane Requirement, no `pass_id` and no rule selector among its inputs.
That context cannot implement [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).4's byte-for-byte
equality with the pre-@3 `Bind` order — which is per-pass — and cannot implement
[W12](#el:w12-call-coherence)'s conjunct 7 or its replay, since both quantify
over a value the context does not determine.

The seam that already resolves it is in the tree and the prior plan pointed at
it without saying so. `internal/driver/catalog.go`'s
`ParseSchedulerBackendPreferences` returns `map[string][]string` **keyed by
pass**, taking the first rule in declared order whose `match` names that pass and
leaving a pass with no such rule on the declaration-rank fallback;
`internal/scheduler/scheduler.go`'s `preferenceRank` then consumes exactly one
pass's list through `ReadyRun.BackendPreference`; and
`internal/scheduler/placement_baseline_test.go` already asserts the repository's
placement **per pass**, as `preferences[passID]`. The order this plan must
reproduce has always carried the parameter the RPL-004 candidate's backend
context dropped.

[EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition) states the reading,
[W5](#el:w5-backend-context).3, .4, .6 and .7 build and check it,
[W12](#el:w12-call-coherence).2 evaluates coherence under it,
[W13](#el:w13-decision-trace).1 records it, and
[W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).7, [W16](#el:w16-guard-objective) and
[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) give it teeth. The reading is recorded in
`outputs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

### F12 — `rule_id` already denotes something else {#el:f12-rule-id-ambiguity}

Recording the matched placement rule runs straight into a name that is taken.
`policy/scheduler.yaml`'s own comment on `placement_rules` says "the matched
rule id enters the decision trace", but the `rule_id` a binding actually carries
is not one: `internal/scheduler/scheduler.go`'s `bindingRuleID` returns one of
four synthesized **rule-class** identifiers — `v1-ranked-first-capable`,
`v2-quality-floor-ranked-first-capable`, `v3-policy-preferred-first-capable`,
`v4-quality-floor-policy-preferred-first-capable` — naming which placement
*algorithm* produced the binding, never which declared rule matched.
`internal/scheduler/evaluation.go` and `internal/driver/session.go` write that
value under the key `rule_id`, and `internal/driver/run_open_recovery_test.go`
asserts it by that name.

So "the matched rule id in the trace" denotes nothing today, and the obvious
place to put it is occupied by a value with a different meaning. This is
[F6](#el:f6-snapshot-ambiguity)'s shape in a second place, and it takes the same
disposition: two senses, two names, no reuse.
[EN9](#el:en9-matched-rule-recording) fixes the split and
[W13](#el:w13-decision-trace).10 states it as a criterion.

### F13 — The pre-@3 policy reader delimits sections by column {#el:f13-line-based-reader}

[F5](#el:f5-incomplete-telemetry) and [F10](#el:f10-influence-declaration) are
about what `policy/scheduler.yaml` declares. This finding is about how the
repository's existing code *reads* it, and it becomes load-bearing the moment the
operative document is edited before the Driver is rewired.

`internal/driver/catalog.go`'s `ParseSchedulerBackendPreferences` does not parse
YAML. It walks lines: `yamlDoc.section("placement_rules")` starts collecting at
the line beginning `placement_rules:` and **stops at the first subsequent line
whose first character is neither a space nor a tab** — that is, at the next
top-level key. Every preference list in this repository is recovered by that
scan, and `internal/scheduler/placement_baseline_test.go` asserts the resulting
placement through it.

The description "the scheduler policy document" therefore denotes one thing, but
"adding a block to it" does not: a `placement_objectives` block declared as a
top-level sibling is invisible to the scan and changes no pass's preference,
while the same content indented under `placement_rules` would be scanned as rule
content. Nothing in the staged tree states this, and the schema cannot catch it —
`schema/scheduler-policy.schema.json` is `additionalProperties: true` at every
level and would accept both.

Two consequences bind [W4](#el:w4-policy-document). Its criterion 8 requires the
new blocks to be top-level siblings and asserts the consequence rather than the
placement: over the delivered bytes the pre-@3 projection must still yield a
preference list for exactly the five passes that carry one today and none for the
other seven. And [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring), which routes the Driver through
the validator, is eleven packets later — so for the whole middle of this graph
the line-based reader is the *only* consumer of the edited document, and
[W5](#el:w5-backend-context).4's per-pass equality is stated through it.

This is a finding against the staged tree rather than against the design: the
design says nothing about how a repository parses its own policy, and it should
not. It is recorded because a lowering that edits the document has to know it.

### F14 — The design names no seam that gives a live placement its evidence {#el:f14-live-path-unnamed}

[E4](#el:w9-evidence-context) says the evidence context "is assembled upstream of
the seam" by "a derived store", [E3](#el:w7-telemetry-fold) says that store folds
ledger facts, and the design's rejected alternatives close off both a live ledger
query at Bind and a per-decision refold — so a *maintained* store, read upstream
of the seam, is the only shape the design leaves standing. What no element states
is the obligation that a placement this repository actually makes takes its
context from that store.

The gap is exact, and it is what the refused candidate fell into. Every element
of the design is satisfied by a delivery in which the fold, the screens, the
assembly, the comparison and the seam all exist and are correct, and the Driver
hands `order` the `disabled` variant forever: the seam is **total** on that
input by [W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).9, `coherent` never mentions evidence by
[W12](#el:w12-call-coherence).4, the trace has a canonical form for exactly that
tag combination by [W13](#el:w13-decision-trace).2, and the two cold-start
equalities of [W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).8 are then not a degradation at all
but the delivery's permanent behaviour. Nothing in the design is violated,
because the design specifies the *machine* and this failure is about the
*calling*.

The obligation therefore comes from one level up, and it is unambiguous there.
The pinned target state's `tuple-merit-is-derived-and-pinned` requires the
snapshot's digest and derivation version to be pinned "into **each** scheduling
decision"; `unearned-effort-demotes` says a dominated higher-effort tuple "is
ordered after the lower-effort tuple **for that pass**", which is a claim about
this repository's placements; and the predicate itself says every
evidence-influenced position is "earned by measured outcomes". A mechanism
nothing calls satisfies none of them.

The design's guard-semantics interface does not close the gap either: it ranges
the five guards over fixtures and says in as many words that
supplying the bind marker, the declaration digest and the projection pin "as data
rather than obtaining them by a live read" is what keeps the guards
fixture-executable. That is right about the *values* and silent about the *path*,
so a guard set conforming to it exactly can be blind to whether any placement
ever happens under evidence.

*Closed by* [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path), which names the maintained store
and its read; [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).8–.11, which make the supply universal
over a wake and the pin a property of the produced decision; and the
delivered-path clauses of [W16](#el:w16-guard-objective)–[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades),
which check it where the evaluator looks. This is stated as a finding rather than
absorbed as an encoding decision because it is not a choice this plan made
freely: the design left a seam unnamed, and a reader comparing the two artifacts
should see that the addition is a lowering into open space rather than a
semantic this plan invented.

### F15 — The design names no source for a live wake's applicable priors {#el:f15-live-prior-source-unnamed}

The design is exact about what priors *are* and what they *do*. The evidence
context carries a `priors` member "as the pinned projection's prior schema
delivers", each entry pinned by `source_pin` to an admitted observation and
scoped to a model position; `priors_in` is the whole applicable list when
`tier2_blockers` is empty and the empty list otherwise; and the first cold-start
rung is an equality against what the pinned projection emits for that list. What
no element states is which admitted observations a *running* wake draws that
list from.

The gap is the same kind as [F14](#el:f14-live-path-unnamed) and it bites in the
same place. Every element of the design is satisfied by a delivery whose live
assembly supplies `priors: []` on every wake: the empty list is a legal state
([W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).4), P-C makes the emitted order `base_order`
byte-for-byte, the trace has a canonical form for it, and both cold-start
equalities hold vacuously. Nothing is violated, because the design specifies what
a prior does once it is in the context and this failure is about how one gets
there. The result is a repository whose placements are permanently on the second
rung while the plan claims a ladder.

The obligation again comes from one level up, and again it is unambiguous. The
pinned predicate says cold start "falls back **first** to an applicable
model-scoped prior and **then** to the declared policy/rank/id order", and the
accepted clause `evidence-cold-degrades` says degradation is to "applicable model
priors and then to the exact declared policy/rank/id order". Two rungs, in that
order. A delivery that reaches the second without ever being able to be on the
first has not degraded deterministically; it has one behaviour wearing the name
of a ladder.

The staged tree makes this concrete rather than theoretical.
`backends/observations/artificial-analysis.json` declares `scope: model` and
carries **fourteen** observations, every one of whose subjects is a key declared
in `backends/observations/fleet-models.yaml` — the model vocabulary that binds
them to the model names the `backends/*/backend.yaml` adapters declare — and
`internal/backend/observations` reads both offline. So an applicable prior is the
ordinary case here, not an edge one, and a candidate set drawn from the accepted
declarations will have priors for most of its members. That is precisely why a
delivered-path guard asserting the `base_order` floor unconditionally would have
been checking a coincidence, and why the fixture that distinguishes the rungs has
to order its candidates differently from `base_order`
([W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8) rather than merely exist.

*Closed by* [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).12, which names the source and makes
`priors: []` a decided state; [EN13](#el:en13-live-prior-source), which records
the location and the two alternatives it was chosen over; and
[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s delivered-path clause with
[W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8's two prior sets, which make both rungs
mechanically distinguishable on the path a wake takes. It is stated as a finding
rather than absorbed for [F14](#el:f14-live-path-unnamed)'s reason: the addition
is a lowering into space the design left open, not a semantics this plan
invented.

## Encoding decisions {#el:encoding}

These are L3 choices about *where code and data live*. None changes a semantic
the design fixes; each is recorded so a reviewer can see that the choice was
made deliberately and see its alternative.

### EN1 — New code lands in the two existing packages, not a new one {#el:en1-package-placement}

Everything new that carries a seam value, a pure function or a whole-ledger
fold is a file in `internal/scheduler/` or `internal/derived/`, both of which
exist. The Driver's own wiring is the exception, by necessity rather than by
preference: a placement is *made* in `internal/driver`, so
[W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring) and [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path) write files
there and are bound by that package's existing dependencies —
`internal/driver` already imports `internal/store`, and may import
`internal/derived`, because neither imports it back. The split below governs the
seam packages, which is where the cycle risk lives. The split follows the
existing dependency direction:
`internal/derived` already imports `internal/scheduler`, never the reverse.
Therefore **value types and pure functions** (the snapshot shape, the evidence
context shape, the thresholds variants, the comparison algebra, the ordering
seam, `coherent`, the trace, and the declaration-context assemblies) go in
`internal/scheduler/`, and the **whole-ledger folds and rebuilds** (the
telemetry fold, derivation agreement, evidence-context assembly) go in
`internal/derived/`, whose package doc already declares it builds "rebuildable
projections from ledger records". A third package was considered and rejected:
it would add a new top-level directory for no semantic gain, and the split above
already keeps the seam pure.

One import invariant makes the split safe and is a packet-level obligation, not
a style note: **no new file imports `internal/store`.** Every ledger fact
arrives as an already-decoded record supplied by the caller, exactly as the
existing `derived.Fold` takes its input today. `internal/records` imports only
`internal/ledger` and `internal/scf`, so `internal/scheduler` may take decoded
records without cycling; were a declaration-context assembly to read the store
instead, it would pull `internal/store` — which imports `internal/derived`,
which imports `internal/scheduler` — into a cycle. The same invariant is what
keeps the seam pure and the guards free of live infrastructure, so it is the
same constraint stated twice rather than two constraints.

### EN2 — The projection is resolved through a registry, not an import {#el:en2-projection-registry}

The ordering seam must apply "the projection identified by
`objective.projection_pin`", and that projection's implementation is
`internal/derived/backend.go`. A direct import from `internal/scheduler` would
invert the existing dependency and cycle. So `internal/scheduler/projection.go`
declares the projection *interface* — the P-A–P-D obligations as a callable
contract plus a pin-keyed registry — and `internal/derived/placement_projection.go`
registers the @2 implementation into it. This is also what makes the guards
fixture-executable: a fixture projection registers the same way, so
`guard-objective-binds-per-pass`'s two-vocabulary fixtures need no accepted head.

### EN3 — The backend context is composed from declarations and the matched placement rule {#el:en3-backend-context-composition}

Resolving [F3](#el:f3-policy-position) and [F11](#el:f11-rule-selector). The
backend declaration context supplies `(harness, model, effort)` and
`declared_rank` from `backends/*/backend.yaml`, and `declared_policy_position` is
the index of the backend id in **one** `placement_rules[].prefer.backends` list
of the operative validated policy — the list of the rule the placed pass
matches — with unlisted backends taking the single position that sorts after
every listed one. Four parts.

- **The context is parameterized by the matched rule, and the rule by the placed
  pass.** The context in force is a function of three things:
  `(ledger position, operative validated policy, matched placement rule)`, and
  the third is `resolve_placement_rule(validated_policy, pass_id)` where
  `pass_id` is the pass the Lane Requirement at `requirement_pin` names.
  Coherence conjunct 3 already pins that `pass_id` to the requirement, so the
  selector is **derived, never carried**: no member is added to the design's
  nine-member placement call, and a replayer holding the trace recomputes the
  selector from values the call already determines. The context stays a total,
  rebuildable, content-addressable function — of a position *and the subject
  being placed*, both of which the ledger fixes.

- **The matching rule is the one the repository already runs.**
  `resolve_placement_rule` is the first rule in declared order whose `match`
  names the placed pass, yielding that rule's `prefer.backends` list; a pass
  matched by no such rule, or by a rule carrying no `prefer.backends`, yields
  the **empty list**, under which every backend takes the unlisted position and
  `base_of` degenerates to ascending `(declared_rank, backend_id)`. That is
  exactly what `ParseSchedulerBackendPreferences` produces today and exactly
  what `preferenceRank` consumes, which is why
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).4's byte-for-byte equality with the pre-@3 `Bind`
  order is reachable at all — and [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).6 states it as an
  equality against that existing function across every cataloged pass rather
  than as a re-derivation that could drift from it.

- **Only `match.pass` is lowered; richer qualifiers are not.** Two live rules
  carry more than a pass in `match` — `gating-review-independent`
  (`{pass: review, gating: true}`) and `harvest-expiring-first`
  (`{mode: harvest}`) — and the existing projection reads neither qualifier: it
  keys on `match.pass` and takes the first rule per pass, so
  `gating-review-independent` never contributes a preference (it declares none)
  and `harvest-expiring-first` is skipped (it names no pass). This plan
  reproduces that behaviour exactly and does not lower the qualifiers, because
  lowering them would change which list a pass matches and therefore re-tune
  placement — which is neither this delivery's work nor an L3 choice. The
  restriction is recorded in [Deferrals](#el:deferrals).

- **Both inputs stay ledger-anchored declared data.** The declarations under
  `backends/` and the operative policy document are both admitted declared data
  of the repository, so the composite is content-addressable at any position and
  C32's "the context in force at `M`" remains a computation rather than an
  assertion. What the selector adds is not authority; it is the second index
  into data that was always two-dimensional.

Two alternatives were considered and rejected. **Making the matched-rule
resolution a fourth declared derivation of the validated policy**, beside
`resolve_objective`, `thresholds_of` and `base_of`, would contradict the
design's enumeration of exactly three, and it would misplace the value: the
three are derivations the *policy binding* authorizes and the load verdict
quantifies over, while the backend context is explicitly **not pinned** and no
load verdict quantifies over it. The design leaves open how a repository
declares positions; it does not leave open how many derivations the binding has.
**Copying each backend's position into its declaration** — a per-backend
`declared_policy_position` field in `backends/*/backend.yaml` — would give the
context a single value with no selector, but it cannot represent a backend that
holds different positions under different rules, which nine live backends do,
and it would duplicate policy into sixty declarations that then drift from it.
It is the reading [F3](#el:f3-policy-position) already rejected, and
[F11](#el:f11-rule-selector)'s inverted pair is why it cannot be rescued.

### EN4 — Two snapshots, two names, two fields {#el:en4-two-snapshots}

Resolving [F6](#el:f6-snapshot-ambiguity): `scheduler.Snapshot` keeps its
present meaning and its present trace member (the capacity pin every `Binding`
already carries). The scheduler-telemetry snapshot is a distinct type in
`internal/scheduler/telemetry_snapshot.go`, reaches the seam only inside the
evidence context, and is recorded in the trace under the evidence basis. No
field is reused and no existing digest changes meaning.

### EN5 — The projection record is a policy document, not a new catalog family {#el:en5-projection-record-home}

The admitted projection record is `policy/placement-projection.yaml`, a single
document with `status: accepted`, validated by
`schema/placement-projection.schema.json`, and pinned by the content address of
its bytes. D0002.C9 places placement concerns in policy, `policy/scheduler.yaml`
already carries `status: accepted`, and one document per record keeps the
content address unambiguous. A new `catalog/placement-projections/` directory
was considered and rejected: it would add a cataloged vocabulary family for a
single record and put a placement concern outside policy.

### EN6 — Compare-and-append reuses the existing conditional append {#el:en6-append-seam}

C34's atomic compare-and-append is not a new mechanism here. `internal/store/ledger.go`
already exposes `AppendRecordIf(opts, admit func(all []records.DecodedRecord) error)`,
whose documented order is lock → recover-and-scan → `Records()` → admit →
seal-if-due → append, with a veto performing **no ledger append of any kind**.
The bind-marker commit is that seam with an admit callback asserting that the
observed head is still the last record: on a veto the construction is discarded
and no record is placed, which is exactly C34's "does not place the record later
— it does not place it at all". The seam denotes uniquely and this plan extends
it rather than adding a second append path.

### EN7 — An outcome sample's completion category is read from the submission it produced {#el:en7-completion-category}

Resolving [F9](#el:f9-completion-mass): the classification of an outcome sample
into `complete`, `partial`, `error` or `abandonment` is a **two-case total
function** of facts the ledger already records, and both cases are fixed by
accepted decisions rather than chosen here.

- **The binding produced a submission.** The category is that submission's own
  declared `status`. `schema/ledger-records/submission_received.schema.json`
  declares `status` as exactly `complete | partial | failed` and carries the
  `dispatch_id`, `lane_id` and `attempt` that name the binding, and RFC 0007
  fixes the mapping onto the RFC 0004 failure modes in its own words —
  `complete` is all required outputs present, `partial` is at least one present
  and at least one missing, and `failed` is no admissible outputs, "maps to
  `pass_error` from the run's perspective". So `complete → complete`,
  `partial → partial`, `failed → error`, with nothing left to decide.
- **The binding produced none.** The category is `abandonment`. RFC 0007 states
  it directly: "`abandonment` is never a status — it is the absence of a
  submission, by construction", and this repository's own how-to names
  abandonment as the mode in which no submission arrived within the dispatch
  horizon.

The two cases are complementary over a single already-recorded fact — did this
binding produce a submission? — so the function is total by construction, in the
same shape as the design's sampled/unsampled predicate and for the same reason.
It needs no enumeration of closure outcomes to be complete, which is what makes
it robust to the case [F9](#el:f9-completion-mass) names: `submitted_late` is a
submission the binding's own execution produced, so it carries a `status` and
lands by the first case; `canceled` and `superseded_by_redispatch` are
terminations the binding's own execution did not produce, so the design's prior
predicate has already routed them to `unsampled` and this function never sees
them.

One property bounds what a misreading of this encoding could cost.
`adverse_completion` is `partial + error + abandonment`, so **every** category
boundary except one is inside the adverse sum: assigning a non-submitting
failure to `error` rather than `abandonment`, or the reverse, cannot move the
dimension the demotion predicate compares. The single boundary that can move it
is `complete` against the other three, and that boundary is fixed by one
declared enum value — `status: complete` — with no case left open. The reading
therefore cannot make `unearned-effort-demotes` decide differently than the
design's own definition of the rate does.

Two alternatives were considered and rejected. **Classifying from the
`pass_run_closed` outcome** puts a run-level record in charge of a
binding-level statistic, which is the grain error the design's fold-unit
definition exists to prevent, and it leaves `submitted_late` undecided.
**Admitting a fifth category or a residual bucket** would mint ontology the
design does not have, would make the four-way rate vocabulary not the vocabulary
the target state names, and would put the vanishing mass back where
[F9](#el:f9-completion-mass) found it, merely under a name.

### EN8 — `influence` is decided at load and never reaches the seam {#el:en8-influence-declaration}

Resolving [F10](#el:f10-influence-declaration): the `influence` declaration is a
**load-time** value with a canonical domain, and it is **not** a member of the
`thresholds` value the seam consumes. Three parts, each chosen to be the
smallest thing that closes the finding.

- **Its domain is the single value `prefer_only`.** The design fixes influence
  as prefer-only for this version outright, so there is no second admissible
  value to choose between and none is invented here. `none`, or any other
  identifier, is out of domain.
- **Its violation is refusal condition 6, not a new condition.** "A declared
  telemetry value lies outside its canonical domain" is already a member of the
  closed eight-condition refusal set, and an out-of-domain `influence` trips it
  exactly as an out-of-domain `min_samples` does. The closed set stays at eight,
  which matters because the design makes closure the reason every consumer
  decides "does this document load" identically.
- **It is optional, so nothing that loads today stops loading.** `influence` is
  *not* a member of the completeness set condition 5 checks; a policy declaring
  a complete telemetry block and no `influence` loads, and means what the design
  says it means. The only documents whose verdict changes are ones that declare
  an influence the accepted predicate does not have.

And it is not carried at the seam. `thresholds_of` yields the `enabled` variant
carrying exactly the design's declared threshold members — the metric, the three
integers and the four margins — so `declaration_digest`, the `thresholds`
argument, the evidence context's member sets and every trace shape are
byte-for-byte what the design fixes and what [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context),
[W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam) and [W13](#el:w13-decision-trace) state. A
declaration that decides whether the document loads at all does not also need to
be an argument of a function that only runs on documents that loaded.

Two alternatives were considered and rejected. **Deleting the line** would make
the completed block the only statement that telemetry is live, which is the
design's semantics exactly — but it leaves the repository with no line a reader
can look at to answer the question, and leaves the next editor free to re-add
`influence: none` to a document where it would again decide nothing. **Making
`influence` a member of the `thresholds` value** would let policy switch
telemetry off without removing the block, which is a second off switch the
design does not have, and it would move `declaration_digest` — changing a
digest the design fixes, for a value the seam never reads.

### EN9 — The matched placement rule is recorded beside `rule_id`, never as it {#el:en9-matched-rule-recording}

Resolving [F12](#el:f12-rule-id-ambiguity): the existing `rule_id` keeps its
present meaning — the synthesized rule *class* `bindingRuleID` returns, naming
which placement algorithm produced the binding — and the matched declared rule
is recorded under a distinct member, `matched_placement_rule`, carrying the
`placement_rules[].id` the placed pass resolved to, or recorded as absent where
the pass matched no rule with a preference list.

The split is the same disposition [EN4](#el:en4-two-snapshots) applies to the
two snapshot senses, and for the same three reasons. Reusing `rule_id` would
silently change the meaning of a value the ledger already holds across every
historical binding, which no reader could then interpret without knowing when it
was written. It would break `internal/driver/run_open_recovery_test.go`, which
asserts the class value by that name. And the two are genuinely independent: a
`build` placement under `build-single-agent-coding` is
`v3-policy-preferred-first-capable` when the run carries no quality floor and
`v4-quality-floor-policy-preferred-first-capable` when it does, with the matched
declared rule the same in both.

The new member is what makes conjunct 7 recomputable against the ledger rather
than merely against the trace. `base_of` is already recomputable from the
per-candidate `declared_policy_position` the trace records; what a replayer
additionally needs is **which list those positions came from**, so it can
re-read the operative document at `bind_marker` and check the equality rather
than accept the recorded numbers. An alternative — recording the matched
`prefer.backends` list itself — was considered and rejected: it duplicates
policy bytes into every decision record, grows without bound with the list (the
live `build` list holds thirty-three entries), and is recoverable from the rule
id and the pinned document anyway.

### EN10 — Nothing reads a validated policy before the document validates {#el:en10-document-before-surface}

Resolving [RPL-005](#el:revision). The rule is one sentence: **a packet that
consumes the resolved policy binding — the objective, the thresholds, or the
exposed placement-rule surface — applies after the packet that makes the
operative document load.** It is an ordering decision, not a semantic one, and it
belongs at L3 because the design fixes what a validated policy yields and leaves
staging to this level.

The rule is forced rather than chosen. [W3](#el:w3-policy-validator).2 makes the
load verdict the *only* gate: a document that trips any member of the closed
eight-condition set produces no binding, and there is no positive load rule and
no partial binding. The staged `policy/scheduler.yaml` trips three conditions at
once. So before [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) there is no validated policy in this
repository at all, and every derivation the binding authorizes is undefined —
not degraded, not empty, undefined.

Three consequences fix the order of the whole upper graph.

- **The document packet's only dependency is the validator.**
  [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) needs to know what "loads" means
  ([W3](#el:w3-policy-validator)) and needs the record it references
  ([W1](#el:w1-projection-record), reached through the validator). It needs
  nothing else — in particular it does not need the backend context, whose
  declared read the prior candidate carried and which was the sole reason the
  document was ordered late.
- **Every other consumer is downstream of it.**
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context) reads the rule surface,
  [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring) resolves the objective,
  [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus) reads the operative document from the subject
  tree, and [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance) and [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)
  assert over it. All five are after [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) in the graph,
  and [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) is the unique upstream member of that set.
- **The validator packet asserts nothing about the operative document.** Its
  subject is the *shape*, exercised over fixtures. A test at
  [W3](#el:w3-policy-validator) asserting that `policy/scheduler.yaml` refuses
  today would be true when written and false one packet later, which is a failing
  gate rather than a check. [W3](#el:w3-policy-validator).12 states this.

One alternative was considered and rejected. **Letting the backend context read
the raw document instead of the validated binding** would make it buildable
before [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) — and would create a second, unvalidated path
to the placement rules, which is exactly the single-validator seam
[W3](#el:w3-policy-validator) exists to establish. The whole point of exposing
the rule surface *on the binding* ([W3](#el:w3-policy-validator).11) is that
there is one way to read policy. Ordering the document first costs nothing and
keeps that at one.

### EN11 — The materialized telemetry store is the Driver's, beside its existing fold snapshot {#el:en11-telemetry-store-location}

Resolving [F14](#el:f14-live-path-unnamed). A snapshot has to exist somewhere
before a wake can read one, and the design says only that it is a derived store.
The staged tree offers three homes, and this plan takes the second.

- **`internal/store`.** The graph fold is materialized here today:
  `internal/store/ledger.go` folds the decoded records at reconcile, writes
  `<graph>/derived/{index,status,claims}.json` and a manifest carrying
  `fold_version` and `export_digest`, and re-derives in shadow under `--verify`.
  Rejected for this fold on two grounds. The store's business is the ledger and
  the graph projection over it; the scheduler-telemetry fold is a *placement*
  concern, and putting it here would make the store import a scheduling
  vocabulary it has no other use for. And `internal/store/ledger.go` is already
  `p12`'s output, so a second writer would put one file in two packets' write
  scopes for no gain.
- **`internal/driver` — taken.** The Driver already maintains exactly this kind
  of store for its own fold: `internal/driver/fold_snapshot.go` persists a
  versioned fold at a named ledger boundary, refuses an incomplete boundary, and
  is read at the start of a wake and replaced at the end of one through the
  graph's derived-file surface, which `internal/driver/session.go` already calls
  for exactly that purpose. The telemetry
  store is the same pattern applied to the fold of [W7](#el:w7-telemetry-fold),
  written in a new file beside it. The consumer is here — a wake is
  `internal/driver`'s `Drive()`, and the placement path
  [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring) names runs through this package — so the read
  crosses no package boundary and needs no new interface. `internal/driver` may
  import `internal/derived`; the reverse import does not exist and would be the
  cycle, so the fold stays where [EN1](#el:en1-package-placement) put it and is
  merely called from here.
- **Nowhere — fold per wake in memory.** Rejected. It is the design's rejected
  "recompute the fold per decision" moved one level up rather than removed: it
  re-derives the whole window on every wake whether or not the ledger moved, it
  gives the digest no existence outside the process that computed it, and it
  makes the rebuild equality of [W17](#el:w17-guard-fold) a claim about a value
  no one can fetch. The design's phrase is "maintaining a derived store", and a
  store that is never stored is not one.

The store is a derived store in the full sense D0002.C2 fixes: rebuildable, never
truth, never authority, and never a fact written onto a declaration. Nothing
reads it to decide eligibility, capacity, a gate, a claim or a deadline, and its
only consumer is the evidence-context assembly.

### EN12 — One evidence context per wake, supplied to every placement {#el:en12-one-context-per-wake}

The design fixes that the screens run "once per assembled evidence context" and
that one context "serves every concurrent placement made under the same
projection and telemetry declarations". It does not name that unit in any
repository. Here the unit is **one `Drive()`** — one wake of
`internal/driver/session.go`.

The wake is the right unit because it is exactly the scope over which the values
a context is bound to are already fixed: the operative policy binding, the
catalog context it was validated against, the backend declarations and the ledger
boundary the Driver read are one set of values for the whole wake, and they are
what the context's `projection_pin` and `declaration_digest` address. A finer
unit — one context per placement — is the per-decision refold the design
rejects, and it would additionally let two placements of the same wake carry two
different context digests for no reason a reader could recover. A coarser unit —
one context for the life of a process — is not wrong in the sense of being
unsound, because a reused context measures a strictly growing freshness lag and
falls out of Tier 1 by arithmetic rather than by anyone noticing; it is rejected
because it makes the ledger boundary the evidence was assembled at drift
arbitrarily far from the one the decision is constructed against, turning an
ordinary Tier 1 state into an accident of process lifetime.

One consequence is worth stating: the context is assembled **before** the wake's
placements and is not re-assembled between them, so two placements of one wake
record the same `context_digest` and may still record different freshness lags,
because the lag is computed at the seam against each decision's own
`bind_marker` ([W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).5). That is the design's separation
working as intended — the context is what was assembled, the lag is where it was
used — and [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s context-reuse case is what checks it.

### EN13 — A live wake's priors are the accepted observation snapshots under `backends/` {#el:en13-live-prior-source}

Resolving [F15](#el:f15-live-prior-source-unnamed). The context's `priors` member
has to be filled from somewhere before the ladder's first rung can be reached,
and the design says only that each entry is pinned to an admitted observation and
delivered by the pinned projection record's prior schema. The staged tree offers
three sources, and this plan takes the first.

- **`backends/observations/` — taken.** It holds exactly what the design
  describes and nothing else:
  `backends/observations/artificial-analysis.json` is a snapshot declaring
  `scope: model`, whose own note records that its entries "describe MODELS, not
  (harness, model, effort) backends; each is a bootstrap prior for every tuple
  over its model, never a tuple measurement", and
  `backends/observations/fleet-models.yaml` is the model vocabulary binding those
  entries to the model keys `backends/*/backend.yaml` declares.
  `internal/backend/observations` is the reader, it is offline by construction
  (`schema/backend-observation.yaml` records that the network is touched only by
  the out-of-band refresh under `tools/refresh-aa-observations`), and
  `LoadOrEmpty` already degrades a missing or unreadable snapshot to the empty
  set, which is the `priors: []` state [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).4 calls
  decided. Every property the live path needs is therefore already true of this
  location: it is in the subject tree, it is accepted rather than derived, it is
  model-scoped and says so, and reading it adds no clock, no network and no
  accepted-head dependence — the same reasons [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).6
  gives for reading the operative policy document.
- **A tuple-scoped read of the telemetry store.** Rejected, and not a near miss:
  it is the scope-widening D0008's predicate forbids in the sentence that admits
  priors at all. Tuple statistics reach the order through the demotion constraint
  and through nothing else ([W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).4); routing them into
  `priors` would put one evidence class in two channels and make `prefer_rank`
  move with a measured statistic, which
  [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s channel-separation fixture exists to catch.
- **A new prior store maintained beside the telemetry store.** Rejected under
  D0001.C12/N11: the accepted concept already has a home, an accepted snapshot
  format, a schema, a reader and a refresh tool. A second producer of the same
  value would be a second thing that can be wrong, and the prior list is
  authority-free evidence whose only obligation is to be the applicable set the
  pinned schema reads — an obligation the existing snapshots already discharge.

Two consequences follow and are stated where they bind. The prior list is
assembled **once per wake** with the rest of the context
([EN12](#el:en12-one-context-per-wake)), so two placements of one wake carry one
prior set and one `context_digest`. And because an applicable prior is the
ordinary case in this repository rather than an edge one, the order a wake emits
is the pinned projection's prior-informed order wherever one applies; the
`base_order` floor is reached only where none does, which is why every
delivered-path equality with that floor in this plan establishes the emptiness
instead of assuming it.

## Ordered work {#el:work}

Twenty-two packets in semantic application order. Each states the seam it
changes as a definite description, the acceptance criteria that decide it is
done, and the verification that decides it mechanically. The design element and
clause ids each packet discharges are named at the head of each item, satisfying
the pass post-invariant that every packet boundary cites design elements.

Element numbers are identities, not positions. [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path)
is [RPL-007](#el:revision-history)'s addition and applies between
[W13](#el:w13-decision-trace) and [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring), which is where it
is written; the twenty-one elements it joins keep the numbers they were reviewed
under, so every anchor a prior ledger cites still resolves to the same work.

### W1 — The admitted objective-projection record {#el:w1-projection-record}

### W2 — Canonical ledger distance and numeric determinism {#el:w2-ledger-distance}

*Discharges:* C11, C19, the `Ledger distance` and `Numeric determinism`
semantics.

**Seam.** The one calculation that governs the lookback window's extent and the
freshness lag, and the representation rules every published statistic obeys.
New files beside the existing scheduler sources, because both the fold
(`internal/derived`) and the seam (`internal/scheduler`) read them and only this
direction avoids a cycle.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. `dist(a, b)` is the count of ledger records strictly after `a` and at or
   before `b`, undefined for `b < a`, with `dist(a, a) = 0`; the ledger origin
   is a named marker preceding every record.
2. The metric carries the single admitted identifier this design version
   declares; an unadmitted identifier is reported, never defaulted.
3. The freshness verdict is the total two-valued function: `beyond_bound` on
   `ill_ordered`, `within_bound` when lag ≤ bound, `beyond_bound` otherwise —
   with the comparison **inclusive** at the boundary and `0` a reachable value.
4. Rates and means are exact rationals as integer numerator and denominator in
   lowest terms; durations are nonnegative integers in the ledger's declared
   resolution; order statistics are nearest rank with no interpolation, median
   at index `⌈n/2⌉` and p90 at `⌈0.9n⌉` over ascending observations indexed
   from 1, defined for every `n ≥ 1` including even `n`.
5. No floating-point value is produced anywhere a value is digested or compared.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` with boundary cases at `dist = 0`, at
the bound exactly, at bound + 1, at the origin, at `b < a`, and with even and
odd observation counts for both order statistics.

### W3 — Scheduler policy shape and the closed refusal-set validator {#el:w3-policy-validator}

*Discharges:* E1, E2, C1, C2, C19, C28, C29 (the three declared derivations),
C30 (the catalog context). *Closes:* the load-verdict half of
[F5](#el:f5-incomplete-telemetry).

**Seam.** The single shared load-time validator every consumer must use, and the
resolved policy binding it produces. The staged tree's policy reader is
`internal/driver/catalog.go` (`LoadSchedulerPolicy`,

function in `internal/scheduler/` so the guards can exercise it without the
Driver, and [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring) routes the Driver through it.

This packet is the *shape*, not the document. Under
[EN10](#el:en10-document-before-surface) it applies before any document in this
repository loads under it, so criterion 12 is as much a part of the packet as the
refusal set is: everything here is exercised over fixture documents, and the
operative document's verdict belongs to [W4](#el:w4-policy-document).

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The `placement_objectives` shape — mandatory `projection: {id, pin}`,
   optional `by_pass`, optional `by_class`, optional `fallback` — and the
   completed `telemetry` block are expressed in
   `schema/scheduler-policy.schema.json`. The schema continues to accept every
   block the operative document already carries — `placement_rules`,
   `independence`, `concurrency`, `deferral`, `redispatch`, `explainability` —
   so this packet narrows nothing that loads today by shape alone.
2. A document loads **exactly when it trips no member** of the closed
   eight-condition refusal set, and there is **no positive load rule**. Every
   condition is decided independently except condition 8, which precedes the
   vocabulary check because without a resolved projection condition 1 is
   undecidable, not false; a document tripping several refuses for all of them.
3. The two totality conditions are independent: a total `by_pass` with no

4. Ambiguity — a cataloged pass matching two or more declared classes — refuses
   independently of totality and is not excused by a declared `fallback`.
5. Every declared value outside its canonical domain refuses, and the least
   admissible value of each (`min_samples: 1`, `freshness_bound: 0`,
   `lookback_window: 1`, margins of `0`) loads.
6. `resolve_objective(policy, pass_id)` is total over the pinned catalog
   context and records `kind` **and** the exact matched selector, with the
   selector present exactly when the kind is `pass` or `class` and absent for
   `fallback`.
7. `thresholds_of(policy)` yields the `enabled` variant carrying exactly the
   declared block's members — the metric, the three integers and the four
   materiality margins — or the `disabled` variant with no members. The
   `enabled` variant is yielded **exactly when** the document declares a
   telemetry block, and the `influence` declaration of (10) is **not** a member
   of either variant.
8. The validator's subject is the pair *(document, catalog context)*, the
   catalog context is exactly the cataloged pass ids and the declared class
   definitions in one canonical serialization, and the output is the resolved
   policy binding: `policy_pin`, `catalog_pin`, `validator_version` and
   `binding_digest` over the three.
9. Identical policy bytes validated against two different catalog contexts yield
   two different `binding_digest` values.
10. **The `influence` declaration has a canonical domain of exactly
    `prefer_only`, and it is optional.** Under [EN8](#el:en8-influence-declaration):
    a telemetry block declaring `influence: prefer_only` loads; one declaring
    any other value — `none` included — **refuses**, and refuses under
    **condition 6** of the closed set, so no ninth condition is minted; a
    telemetry block declaring no `influence` at all **loads**, because the
    member is not in condition 5's completeness set; and the value reaches
    neither variant of `thresholds_of`, so no digest, member set or trace shape
    depends on it. The three cases are asserted as three verdicts, not as one.
11. **The validated policy exposes the declared placement-rule surface,
    unchanged and unjudged.** The resolved binding makes
    `placement_rules` — each rule's `id`, its `match` and its
    `prefer.backends` list — readable in **declared order** from the validated
    document, because the backend-context assembly of
    [W5](#el:w5-backend-context) resolves the matched rule from it under
    [EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition). The surface is *carried, not
    judged*: **no ninth refusal condition is minted**, the closed eight-condition
    set of (2) is unchanged, and no document's load verdict moves — a malformed
    or empty rule list loads exactly as it does today, and what it produces is a
    backend context, not a refusal. The three declared derivations stay three;
    the matched-rule resolution is [W5](#el:w5-backend-context)'s, for the reason
    [EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition) records. The surface is
    **reachable only through the binding**: there is no second accessor that
    reads rules from an unvalidated document, which is what makes this the single
    validator seam rather than one of two.
12. **This packet asserts nothing about the operative document's verdict or
    surface.** Under [EN10](#el:en10-document-before-surface) the staged
    `policy/scheduler.yaml` refuses — on conditions 5, 6 and 8 together — and
    [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) is the packet that changes that. So no test
    this packet writes reads the operative document's load verdict, its resolved
    binding or its exposed rule surface: such a test would be true when written
    and false one packet later, and would take the delivery's own `go test ./...`
    gate down with it. The document is read here only as the *shape* criterion 1
    must keep accepting. The live rule-order equality is
    [W4](#el:w4-policy-document).9's, run where a validated document exists.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` over the full refusal set, both
totality conditions at their overlap document, the ambiguity case with and
without a fallback, every domain endpoint, both projection vocabularies from
[W1](#el:w1-projection-record), the two-catalog binding-digest inequality, and
the three `influence` cases of (10) — `prefer_only` loading, `none` refusing on
condition 6 and on no other, absence loading — together with an assertion that
the `thresholds` value serialized from a document carrying `influence` is
byte-identical to the one serialized from the same document without it, and an
assertion that the exposed rule surface of (11) reproduces a **fixture**
document's declared rule order with no rule dropped, reordered or merged,
including a fixture whose rules exercise the multi-rule shapes

### W5 — Declared tuple coordinates and the backend declaration context {#el:w5-backend-context}

*Discharges:* C32, the `base_of` derivation of C29, the effort-sibling grouping
E5 depends on. *Closes:* [F2](#el:f2-backend-positions) and
[F11](#el:f11-rule-selector), under
[EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition) and
[EN10](#el:en10-document-before-surface).

**Seam.** The accepted backend declarations, which must declare the canonical
tuple coordinates as positions rather than leave the model and the effort
implicit in adapter argv; and the assembly of the backend context in force at a
ledger position **for a placed pass**, together with the matched-rule resolution
that selects the one preference list a policy position is an index into.

The parameterization is the whole of [F11](#el:f11-rule-selector) and is why

repository already places by, rather than to a re-derivation that could drift.

**The document this packet reads is the delivered one, and that is
[RPL-005](#el:revision)'s disposition.** Criterion 3 takes an *operative
validated policy* as an input and criteria 4, 6 and 7 quantify over the rule
surface that policy exposes; under [EN10](#el:en10-document-before-surface) a
refused document has neither. So this packet applies after
[W4](#el:w4-policy-document), which is the packet that makes
`policy/scheduler.yaml` load. What that costs is nothing, and the reason is worth
stating precisely rather than asserting, because it is the whole of the
reordering's soundness:

- **"Pre-@3" names the scheduler implementation, not the document.** Criterion
  4's baseline is the ordering `internal/scheduler/scheduler.go` produces —

  `resolve_placement_rule` and `ParseSchedulerBackendPreferences` are compared

  invariant under [W4](#el:w4-policy-document)'s edit by construction, not by
  permission.
- **The rule set is provably unchanged.** [W4](#el:w4-policy-document).7 forbids
  its own packet from touching `placement_rules`, and .8 checks it three ways

  witnesses, which are properties of that block, survive verbatim.
- **Reading the delivered document is strictly better than reading the staged
  one.** The equalities become statements about the document the repository will
  actually place by. [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) re-asserts the same per-pass
  sweep later as a registered guard, so the property is checked twice over the
  same bytes rather than once on each side of an edit.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. `schema/execution-backend.schema.json` declares the coordinate triple, and
   every accepted declaration under `backends/` carries `harness`, `model` and
   `effort` positions and its declared rank. Model names stay inside `backends/`,

2. The declared coordinates agree with the adapter command each declaration
   already pins; a declaration whose declared coordinates and pinned adapter
   disagree is reported, so the new declaration cannot drift from the thing it
   describes.
3. **The backend context is a function of three pinned inputs, and the third is
   the matched placement rule.** The context is a total, rebuildable,

   operative validated policy, and the **matched placement-rule selector**

   document [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) delivered; the assembly reads the rule
   surface **only** through that binding and never from raw bytes, which is what
   keeps the single-validator seam at one.
4. `base_of(eligible)` is the ascending lexicographic order on

6. **`resolve_placement_rule(validated_policy, pass_id)` is total over the

   the matched `placement_rules[].id` **or** its declared absence, and the list
   it yields is **exactly** the list

   refusal set.

### W6 — The telemetry snapshot shape {#el:w6-snapshot-shape}

*Discharges:* E3's published shape, C5, C19, C22. *Applies:*
[EN4](#el:en4-two-snapshots).

**Seam.** The value the fold publishes and the screen reads: a version-invariant
envelope and a version-governed body.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The envelope — `derivation_version`, `distance_metric`, `window {from,to}`,
   `ledger_high_water` — is encoded in a form fixed across every derivation
   version of this lineage and is readable **without** implementing the claimed
   version.
2. The body carries `cells`, the snapshot-level `source_root`, `accounting`, the
   snapshot-level `accounting_root`, and the always-present
   `unattributed_admission_outcomes` group with **both** `n` and
   `unattributed_root`, all in canonical scope order, plus the `digest` over
   envelope and body.
3. **Presence follows the count.** Each of the three dimension groups is always
   present with its own `n`; its value members are present exactly when `n ≥ 1`
   and are *omitted* — never null, never zero, never synthesized — otherwise,
   and the omission is inside the serialization the digest covers.
4. A cell exists exactly when `samples ≥ 1`. An accounting entry exists exactly
   when the scope had an accounted execution, `unsampled` is always present
   including as `0`, and the two structural conditions hold: every published
   cell's scope has an entry, and an entry with `unsampled: 0` has a cell.
5. **The four completion outcomes are an exhaustive partition of `samples`.**
   In every published cell, each of `complete`, `partial`, `error` and
   `abandonment` is the exact rational of its own count over the cell's
   `samples` in lowest terms; each rate multiplied by `samples` is therefore a
   nonnegative integer; the four induced counts **total `samples` exactly**; and
   the four rates **sum to exactly `1`**. A body in which the four rates sum to
   anything other than `1`, or in which any rate does not resolve to an integer
   count over `samples`, is **not well-formed** — the equality is enforced, not
   merely asserted, and its violation is the condition that makes a body
   ill-formed. Two consequences are the point of stating it here rather than
   only in the fold: the counts are recoverable from the published body alone,
   so the condition is decidable without any ledger read and without publishing
   a new member, root or digest scope; and `adverse_completion`, which must
   still equal the published `partial + error + abandonment`, is thereby
   `1 − complete` and ranges over the whole sampled mass rather than over
   whatever mass the producer chose to categorize. This condition is additional
   to — never a replacement for — the design's stated bound: a body summing to
   exactly `1` sums to at most `1`, so every domain the design declares holds.
6. Body well-formedness is decidable only at an implemented version, and the
   full condition list is enforced: nonnegative integer counts, rates exact
   rationals in `[0,1]`, the completion partition of (5), `adverse_completion`
   equal to the published `partial + error + abandonment`, every per-dimension
   `n ≤ samples`, nonnegative finite durations, a cost mean with a declared unit
   exactly when its `n ≥ 1`, and one cell and one entry per scope.
7. The non-sample count is published as **`unsampled`**. No member name, and no
   sentence of the delivered code or docs, uses the artifact-lifecycle word
   family D0005.C1 fixes for this classification.
8. The canonical empty root has one lawful published form fixed by the
   derivation version.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` asserting byte-identical
serialization from two independent constructions of the same value, every
presence rule in both directions, and every well-formedness condition at its
boundary — including a cell one sample's worth of mass short of `samples`, one
a sample's worth over, and one whose rate does not resolve to an integer count,
each of which must be rejected, and the lawful cell at the equality, which must
not be. Mechanically re-exercised by [W17](#el:w17-guard-fold).

### W7 — The scheduler-telemetry fold {#el:w7-telemetry-fold}

*Discharges:* E3, C3, C4, C5, C36. *Serves:* the `merit-observed` conjunct.

**Seam.** The derived, rebuildable projection of the append-only ledger into the
[W6](#el:w6-snapshot-shape) shape, beside the existing graph fold in
`internal/derived/fold.go`.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. **The unit of observation is the Lane Binding, not the pass run.** A pass run
   bound to three lanes contributes three accounted executions to three scopes,
   each carrying its own termination category, its own observed-active latency
   and its own reported cost. A fold that yields one sample for such a run is
   non-conforming.
2. Exactly one class of binding is excluded, identified **structurally** by its
   producing record's re-stamp production mode. Exclusion is never predicated on
   whether a termination carried an admission judgment: a binding closing as
   error, abandonment or partial before admission review **is** an outcome
   sample.
3. Every remaining accounted execution is classified by **one predicate over one
   fact** — whether the binding's recorded termination is one its own execution
   produced. The two sides are complements, so an execution carried under a
   recorded cause outside any enumeration still lands in `unsampled`. The
   classification is derived; nothing is written onto a Lane Binding and nothing
   is appended about it.
4. **The completion classification of the sampled side is total, and its mass is
   never short.** Every outcome sample is assigned **exactly one** of `complete`,
   `partial`, `error` and `abandonment` by the total function of
   [EN7](#el:en7-completion-category), so the four counts of a published cell
   total its `samples` and the four rates it publishes satisfy
   [W6](#el:w6-snapshot-shape).5 by construction. There is no fifth category, no
   residual bucket, no discard and no default. Three prohibitions carry the
   rule, because each names a way the mass has been observed to leak:
   - **No sample is left uncategorized.** A sample counted in `samples` and in
     none of the four is the defect [F9](#el:f9-completion-mass) names; a fold
     that produces one is non-conforming.
   - **A termination the assignment does not cover produces no snapshot.** The
     fold does not drop the execution, does not move it to `unsampled` — the
     prior predicate, not category coverage, decides that side — and does not
     invent a category. It publishes **no snapshot for that window**, so the
     candidate is absent, the screen records `not_available`, and the ladder
     degrades from Tier 2 with the bind still happening. Silence about a
     classification the fold could not make is the one outcome forbidden.
   - **Category assignment reads binding-grain facts only.** The submission that
     decides the category is the one that binding produced, identified by the
     dispatch, lane and attempt the submission record already carries; no
     run-level closure outcome is read as a binding's category, for the same
     grain reason criterion 1 states.
5. An unsampled execution contributes to no rate, no latency observation and no
   cost observation, and is published in its scope's accounting entry.
6. Window membership is decided per folded unit on **one designated record**:
   for an accounted binding its own termination record; for an unattributed
   admission outcome its own admission-outcome record. The two rules are
   disjoint, so nothing is folded twice and nothing lacks a rule. An included
   binding contributes all of its facts, including those preceding the window
   start.
7. Admission outcomes attach at **submission grain**, so each adjudication
   enters exactly one cell's `quality.n`; one adjudicating no single submission
   enters no cell and is counted in `unattributed_admission_outcomes`.
8. Three root families, none derived from another: a per-cell `source_root` over
   the facts folded into that cell; a per-scope `accounting_root` over the
   termination record of every accounted execution of that scope; and the
   snapshot's `unattributed_root` over the admission-outcome record of every
   unattributed outcome — each the multiset of content addresses in ascending
   byte order with repeats retained.
9. The key is never widened and never narrowed: no pooling across harness
   siblings, across effort siblings or across passes, and no projection to a
   model-only claim.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` with the three-lane fixture in both
dispositions, the window boundary at one record inside and one record outside,
the cause-outside-the-enumeration case, a fixture pair whose published
aggregates are identical while the folded fact set differs (the digest must
differ), the completion partition asserted at every published cell over a
fixture holding all four categories including a submitting failure and a
non-submitting one, and the unmapped-termination fixture yielding **no
snapshot** rather than a deficient cell. Mechanically re-exercised by
[W17](#el:w17-guard-fold).

### W8 — The snapshot admissibility screen and derivation agreement {#el:w8-snapshot-screen}

*Discharges:* C24, C27, C35, C20.

**Seam.** The three screens standing between a candidate snapshot and the
evidence context, and the canonical exclusion record they produce. The
declaration-relative half is pure and lives in `internal/scheduler/`; the
ledger-reading rebuild lives in `internal/derived/`.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. **Admissibility** (declaration-relative): `window.to` equals
   `ledger_high_water`; `distance_metric` equals the operative declaration's;
   `derivation_version` is one the consumer implements; and the extent measured
   **under the operative metric** equals the operative `lookback_window`, or
   `window.from` is the origin and the extent is at most it.
2. **Derivation agreement**: folding the ledger at the candidate's **own**
   envelope inputs reproduces its `digest`, every per-cell source root, every
   per-scope accounting root, both snapshot-level roots and the
   `unattributed_root` byte-for-byte. It is decided from the candidate and the
   ledger alone and is therefore independent of (1); a candidate failing both
   records both.
3. The exclusion record is **every applicable reason in one canonical order** —
   `not_available`, `envelope_unreadable`, `derivation_version_unsupported`,
   `metric_mismatch`, `window_mismatch`, `ill_formed`, `derivation_mismatch` —
   with the candidate digest present exactly when candidate bytes existed and
   the observed envelope present exactly when the envelope read.
4. `ill_formed` is **withheld** exactly when `derivation_version_unsupported`
   fires, because body well-formedness is not decidable at an unimplemented
   version; `derivation_mismatch` is likewise withheld where the version or the
   metric is one the consumer does not implement.
5. **The completion-mass equality is a well-formedness condition and reports as
   one.** A candidate whose four completion rates do not sum to exactly `1`, or
   whose rates do not resolve to integer counts over `samples`, is excluded with
   `ill_formed` — **no new exclusion reason is minted**, the closed ordered set
   of (3) is unchanged, and `ill_formed` is withheld at an unimplemented version
   for this condition exactly as for every other body condition. The check is
   decided from the published body alone, so it needs no ledger read and no
   rebuild.
6. **Well-formedness and derivation agreement are decided independently, and
   agreement does not certify completion mass.** A fold and a rebuild that omit
   the same completion mass agree on every root and on the digest, so (2)
   accepts them; (5) excludes the candidate anyway. This is the ordering the
   defect [F9](#el:f9-completion-mass) records depends on: a consistent omission
   is a well-formedness failure, never an agreement failure, and only a screen
   that decides both can see it.
7. **Nothing refuses.** Every failure produces an `excluded` slot and the ladder
   proceeds from Tier 2. A fabricated-but-shape-valid candidate is excluded and
   the bind still happens; so is a deficient-mass candidate, whose exclusion
   costs the placement its Tier 1 evidence and nothing else.
8. The screen runs **once per assembled evidence context**, not once per
   decision.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` over the exact/shorter/longer/origin-
truncated window cases, the multiply-invalid candidate's exact reason list, the
fabricated-but-shape-valid candidate, a single-fact perturbation that leaves
every aggregate unchanged, and the agreeing-but-deficient candidate, whose
reason list must be exactly `[ill_formed]` while derivation agreement holds.
Mechanically re-exercised by [W17](#el:w17-guard-fold) and
[W19](#el:w19-guard-prefer-only).

### W9 — The evidence context {#el:w9-evidence-context}

*Discharges:* E4's context, C17, C23, C25.

**Seam.** The seam's single evidence input, in its two canonical variants, and
its assembly.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The `enabled` variant has exactly six members: `telemetry`,
   `projection_pin`, `declaration_digest`, the two-variant `snapshot` slot,
   `priors`, `context_digest`. The `disabled` variant has exactly four:
   `telemetry`, `projection_pin`, `priors`, `context_digest`.
2. **No head marker, no freshness lag and no freshness verdict is a member of
   either variant.** There is no `distance_metric` member and no `no_snapshot`
   value. A context carrying any of them is ill-formed.
3. `declaration_digest` is the content address of the `enabled` `thresholds`
   value itself, tag included, under the same canonical serialization the seam
   digests its `thresholds` argument under — so the equality is like-for-like by
   construction and no separate reconciliation rule exists.
4. `priors: []` is a state, not a missing input, in both variants.
5. Two independent builders produce the same `context_digest` for the same
   prior set, projection pin and slot — including for the `disabled` variant and
   including for an `excluded` slot.
6. The context is **pass-independent and decision-independent**: one assembled
   context lawfully serves every concurrent placement made under the same
   projection and telemetry declarations.
7. **Both variants are decided, and neither is a default.** `disabled` is
   produced from an operative validated policy that declares no `telemetry`
   block, and an `excluded` slot from a screen that excluded the candidate the
   store returned or from the store returning none. This seam offers no
   constructor for a context that stands for "assembly was not attempted", so a
   caller cannot express one. Making that a property of the delivered tree rather
   than of this seam is [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).8–.9 and .12's work; stating
   it here is what makes the two halves one rule.
8. **The variant and the slot are decided on two independent axes, and the
   assembly cannot cross them.** This is (7) made checkable, and it is
   [RPL-008](#el:revision)'s correction.
   - The **variant** is a function of the validated policy alone. An operative
     policy declaring a `telemetry` block yields an `enabled` context **whatever
     the store returned**, including when it returned nothing. `disabled` has
     exactly one producer — a validated policy with no `telemetry` block — and no
     store state, read failure or screen verdict is one. There is no code path
     from an absent snapshot to a `disabled` context.
   - The **slot** is a function of what the store returned and what the screens
     said, and of nothing else. With no candidate bytes the reason list is
     exactly `[not_available]`, the first member of the closed ordered set, and
     the `candidate_digest` and `observed_envelope` members are absent because
     neither existed — the design's own total construction, adopted unchanged and
     minting no reason.
   - Neither axis touches `priors`. The context's prior list is what the pinned
     projection's prior schema delivers over the applicable admitted
     observations, and it is a member of **both** variants, so no policy state
     and no store state can empty it; only the absence of an applicable
     observation does that. (What reaches `prefer` is `priors_in`, which
     [W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).2 empties on a Tier 2 blocker — a seam-side
     rule about a carried list, not an assembly-side rule about what is carried.)
     This is what keeps the first cold-start rung reachable from every state of
     the second.

   Two independent builders given the same policy, the same store result and the
   same applicable observations produce the same variant, the same slot and the
   same `context_digest`, so the rule is decided by the inputs rather than by the
   assembler.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` asserting the exact member sets of
both variants, digest agreement across two builders, and that no marker, lag or
verdict can be serialized; and, for (8), a matrix over {policy declares
telemetry, policy declares none} × {store returns an admissible candidate, store
returns a candidate the screens exclude, store returns nothing} × {an applicable
prior exists, none does}, asserting the variant, the slot, the exact reason list
and the prior list of each of the twelve cells — the two enabled-policy
no-candidate cells asserting `enabled` with a slot reason list of exactly
`[not_available]`, and all six disabled-policy cells asserting the same
four-member `disabled` serialization whatever the store returned, each with its
prior list intact. Mechanically re-exercised by
[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades).

### W10 — The comparison algebra and the demotion predicate {#el:w10-comparison}

*Discharges:* E5's predicate, C7, C8, C9, C16. *Requires:*
[W3](#el:w3-policy-validator) for the thresholds API, [W6](#el:w6-snapshot-shape)
for the statistics it compares, [W2](#el:w2-ledger-distance) for the numeric
rules it compares them under.

**Seam.** The closed per-dimension comparison rules and the three-condition
predicate that produces the constraint set `D` over effort-sibling groups.

Both of the packet's declared thresholds inputs come from
[W3](#el:w3-policy-validator) and from nowhere else: `min_samples`, which
criterion 1 reads to decide determinability, and the four materiality margins,
which criterion 4 compares strictly against, reach this seam only as the
`thresholds` value `thresholds_of` yields over a validated document. The
comparison never reads a policy document itself and never carries a default for
an undeclared margin, so it cannot be built before the validator exists. This
is the dependence RPL-001 found missing from the work graph.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. Determinability is per dimension and requires that dimension's own `n` to
   reach `min_samples`; because `min_samples ≥ 1` is enforced at load, a
   dimension whose values are omitted is `not determinable` **before any value
   is consulted**.
2. Condition 1 requires Tier 1 availability and both cells present, each holding
   at least `min_samples` outcome samples. Condition 2 requires the quality
   comparison to be **determinable** and shows no advantage beyond
   `quality_advantage` on either gate-refusal or needs-revision. Condition 3
   requires at least one of latency, cost and adverse completion to be
   determinable and materially worse.
3. **Absence is never read as "no advantage."** An unmeasured quality dimension
   blocks demotion; it does not permit it.
4. Every boundary comparison is **strict**: a value sitting exactly on the
   margin (`h = l × (1+m)`, `h = l + m`, `h = l − m`) does not demote, including
   at a declared margin of `0`.
5. Latency demotes only when **both** median and p90 are worse; a zero
   lower-effort value on a ratio dimension is not comparable; cost is not
   compared across two declared units, and a cell having more reporting
   executions does not by itself change the comparison.
6. `D` is acyclic by construction: pairs exist only within one effort-sibling
   group and only in the direction of increasing effort.
7. The adverse-completion dimension is compared **as published**, and the
   comparison neither recomputes it nor repairs it. It is sound to do so only
   because [W6](#el:w6-snapshot-shape).5 closes the completion partition and
   [W8](#el:w8-snapshot-screen).5 excludes any cell that does not: a rate whose
   numerator lost observations is not the rate this predicate names, and the
   right place to stop it is the screen, not a compensation here. The seam reads
   only admitted evidence, so this packet asserts nothing about deficient cells
   beyond that it never sees one.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` with a fixture at each named
non-demotion condition and at each margin boundary from both sides.
Mechanically re-exercised by [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance).

### W11 — The ordering seam {#el:w11-ordering-seam}

*Discharges:* E4, E5's extension, E6, C6, C10, C11, C12, C18, C21, C31.

**Seam.** The one function that converts evidence into order:
`order(pass_id, bind_marker, eligible, objective, evidence_context, thresholds,
base_order) -> permutation(eligible)`.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The codomain is permutations of the input. The seam cannot add, remove,
   duplicate or annotate a candidate, and emits no gate result, claim, deadline,
   capacity change or filter outcome. Prefer-only is a property of the type.
2. `order = refine ∘ prefer ∘ base`. `base` is `base_of(eligible)` and is
   determined, not supplied. `prefer` is the pinned projection applied to
   `(objective.id, eligible, priors_in, base_order)` **and nothing else**, where
   `priors_in` is the whole applicable prior list when `tier2_blockers` is empty
   and the empty list otherwise. No tuple statistic and no tier mask reaches
   `prefer`.
3. `refine` is the **unique lexicographically least** linear extension of `D`
   under `prefer_rank`. S1–S4 hold: determinacy, satisfaction of every pair,
   identity when nothing binds — including when `D` is nonempty and already
   satisfied — and displacement confined to demoted candidates.
4. Tier is resolved **per candidate and per dimension**, never as one scalar,
   and the classification routes nothing: Tier 1 acts only through the demotion
   constraint, Tier 2 only through `prefer`, Tier 3 through no channel.
5. Tier 1 availability is the conjunction of both tags `enabled`, the
   declaration digests equal, an `admitted` slot, and a `within_bound` verdict
   the seam computes from the snapshot's high-water mark, the `bind_marker`
   argument and the `thresholds` argument's own `freshness_bound`.
6. The freshness lag and verdict exist **exactly** where the slot is `admitted`
   **and** the `thresholds` argument is `enabled`. Everywhere else no lag and no
   verdict is computed, recorded or invented — including on the mixed call whose
   `admitted` slot faces `disabled` thresholds, whose Tier 1 blocker list is
   exactly `[thresholds_disabled]` and never contains `snapshot_not_fresh`.
7. Blockers are recorded as **canonically ordered lists** of every applicable
   member, never one chosen reason: Tier 1 is `context_disabled`,
   `thresholds_disabled`, `declaration_mismatch`, `snapshot_excluded`,
   `snapshot_not_fresh` in that order; Tier 2 is `projection_mismatch`. Each list
   is empty exactly when its tier is available.
8. **The two cold-start equalities hold.** With no candidate Tier 1 anywhere the
   emitted order is byte-for-byte what the pinned projection emits for the same
   inputs; with additionally no applicable prior it is `base_order`
   byte-for-byte, which P-C makes checkable. Telemetry-block absence disables
   Tier 1 only and never bypasses the prior rung.
9. Nothing in the ladder can refuse a bind, raise an error, or leave the seam
   undefined on any input; the function is total on all four tag combinations.
10. Under-sampling never borrows from a harness sibling, an effort sibling or
    another pass.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` covering all four tag combinations,
both cold-start equalities, the intervening-candidate extension case
`[higher, X, lower]` emitting exactly `[X, lower, higher]`, an already-satisfied
nonempty `D`, and per-dimension mixed tiering. Mechanically re-exercised by
[W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance), [W19](#el:w19-guard-prefer-only) and
[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades).

### W12 — Call coherence and the atomic append {#el:w12-call-coherence}

*Discharges:* E10, C29, C30, C32, C33, C34. *Applies:*
[EN6](#el:en6-append-seam).

**Seam.** The `coherent` precondition on a placement call, and the commit by
which a decision obtains its `bind_marker` — the latter being the existing
conditional append in `internal/store/ledger.go`.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The placement call is the nine-member tuple; the seam consumes seven of them.
   `policy_binding` and `requirement_pin` are members of the call and **not**
   arguments of `order`, because the seam does not read them.
2. `coherent` is total and two-valued over its seven conjuncts, every one of
   which relates a carried value to something **outside** the call: the policy
   loads against the pinned catalog context; `catalog_pin` recomputes equal to
   the catalog context in force at `bind_marker`; `pass_id` is the pass the Lane
   Requirement at `requirement_pin` names; `objective`, `thresholds` and
   `base_order` are images of that one validated policy; and every candidate's
   five carried values equal what the backend context in force at `bind_marker`
   declares for its `backend_id`.

   **Conjunct 7 is evaluated under the matched-rule selector, which is derived
   and not carried.** Under [EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition) the
   backend context is a function of the position *and* the matched placement
   rule, and that rule is `resolve_placement_rule` of the validated document at
   `policy_pin` applied to `pass_id`. Both inputs are already members of the
   call and conjunct 3 already pins `pass_id` to the Lane Requirement, so the
   selector adds **no tenth member** to the nine-member call, no new pin, and no
   value a caller could assert. It is a value the replayer recomputes at the
   same position, exactly as `catalog_pin` is. The consequence is a new unlawful
   case with the same shape as the existing single-value substitutions: a call
   whose candidates carry the positions of a **different rule's** list — for
   instance the `build-single-agent-coding` positions on a call placing a
   `proposal-generation` requirement — fails conjunct 7 and produces no Lane
   Binding, even though every position it carries is a real position declared by
   the same document.
3. An unlawful call produces **no Lane Binding**, surfacing through the existing
   closed escalation vocabulary as `binding_unsatisfiable`. No new code is
   minted.
4. **No evidence value appears in `coherent`.** Varying the evidence context
   arbitrarily never makes a lawful call unlawful, and the seam's emitted order
   is identical for a coherent and an incoherent call over the same seven
   arguments.
5. `bind_marker` is the ledger head the construction **observed**, never a
   predicted, reserved or chosen position. The commit succeeds only if the
   deciding record lands at that head's immediate successor; on an intervening
   append **no record is placed at all** and the construction is redone against
   the new head.
6. `dist(bind_marker, position of the carrying record) = 1`. Marker equality
   with the carrying record's own position is a failure, not a variant reading.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` with a cross-policy substitution, a
supplied `base_order` differing from `base_of`, each of the four single-value
substitutions (rank only, policy position only, one tuple coordinate only, an
undeclared backend), the **wrong-rule substitution** of (2) — one document, two
passes, the other pass's positions carried, with `base_order` recomputed over
them so every other equality still holds — a pass substitution, a catalog
movement, an intervening append, and a marker-equality trace that must fail.
Mechanically re-exercised by [W16](#el:w16-guard-objective).

### W13 — The scheduling decision trace {#el:w13-decision-trace}

*Discharges:* E7, C13, C26. *Closes:* [F7](#el:f7-lane-binding-open) and

**Seam.** The record shape the decision is written into — the existing

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The trace carries the policy binding's four members, the requirement pin, the

   `matched_placement_rule`, carrying the `placement_rules[].id` the placed pass
   resolved to under [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).6, or recorded as absent where
   the pass matched no rule carrying a preference list. The two selectors are
   independent and are recorded as two members: the objective's selector names a
   key in `placement_objectives`, the rule selector names a rule in
   `placement_rules`, and a trace recording one for the other fails. The rule
   selector is what makes conjunct 7 recomputable against the ledger rather than
   only against the recorded numbers, for the reason
   [EN9](#el:en9-matched-rule-recording) gives.
2. **Two independent variant tags**, `context_variant` and
   `thresholds_variant`; `evidence_basis` is shaped by the first and the

3. The `freshness` block is present **exactly** where `context_variant` is

4. Both blocker lists are recorded as canonically ordered lists recomputable

5. Every candidate records its `backend_id`, its three tuple positions, its two

6. `demotions` **is** the constraint set `D`: every pair the predicate produced

7. An `excluded` slot is recorded with its ordered reason list, its candidate

8. `schema/ledger-records/lane_binding.schema.json` declares the members the

9. The two snapshot senses are recorded under distinct members and neither

10. **The two rule-identity senses are likewise distinct.** Under

### W22 — The live evidence path: the materialized telemetry store and its read {#el:w22-live-evidence-path}

*Discharges:* E3's store side, E4's context where it is actually produced, C3,
C5's pin, C20. *Closes:* [F14](#el:f14-live-path-unnamed) and the materialization
half of [RPL-007](#el:revision-history). *Applies:*
[EN11](#el:en11-telemetry-store-location). *Requires:*
[W7](#el:w7-telemetry-fold) for the fold it runs, [W6](#el:w6-snapshot-shape) for
the value it stores, and [W12](#el:w12-call-coherence) for the ledger surface it
reads.

**Seam.** The place derived tuple evidence becomes available to a running Driver:
a maintained, content-addressed scheduler-telemetry store, materialized from
ledger facts and read at a wake. In the staged tree the Driver already maintains
exactly one store of this kind — `internal/driver/fold_snapshot.go`, which
persists a versioned fold at a named ledger boundary under the graph directory,
refuses an incomplete boundary and re-folds when the ledger has advanced — and
this element adds the second, in a new file beside it, for the fold of
[W7](#el:w7-telemetry-fold). It does **not** write `internal/store/ledger.go`:
the records the fold consumes are the ones that file's existing whole-ledger read
already returns, and the fold takes them as an argument, exactly as
`derived.Fold` does today.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. **Materialization.** The store holds at most one snapshot per graph: the fold
   of [W7](#el:w7-telemetry-fold) over the ledger facts up to the boundary the
   Driver read, written together with its digest, its derivation version and its
   version-invariant envelope. The written bytes are a function of the folded
   facts alone — no clock reading, no process identity and no accepted head
   enters them.
2. **Refresh, not mutation.** The store is re-derived when the ledger has
   advanced past the boundary the stored snapshot names, and is never updated in
   place from a partial observation. This is the shape the design's two rejected
   alternatives leave: the fold runs at refresh rather than at Bind, and the
   screens run once per assembled context rather than once per decision, so
   neither a live query at Bind nor a per-decision refold exists on this path.
3. **One read, four degraded states, and none of them is a policy state.** A
   wake obtains the current candidate snapshot, or the fact that there is none,
   by one read. Absence, an unreadable or truncated body, a claimed derivation
   version this consumer does not implement, and a boundary ahead of the ledger
   the wake read are four **distinct observed states**, and every one of them is
   a degradation: each still yields something the assembly of
   [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context) turns into a context the wake's placements use,
   and none refuses a bind, raises an error, halts a wake, or repairs the store.
   That is C20 on the live path.

   **Under an operative policy declaring a `telemetry` block, all four produce an
   `enabled` context with an `excluded` slot** — the first with a reason list of
   exactly `[not_available]` and no candidate digest or observed envelope, the
   other three with the reason each screen assigns and with the candidate digest
   and observed envelope they retained. None of the four produces a `disabled`
   context, because `disabled` is what a policy declares and not what a store
   reports ([W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).8). This is
   [RPL-008](#el:revision)'s correction stated where the states are enumerated.
   And none of the four touches the prior list: a wake with no store at all still
   carries its applicable priors and is on the ladder's first rung, not its
   floor.
4. **Never authority.** Nothing reads this store to decide eligibility,
   capacity, a gate result, a claim, a deadline or a lane count. Its only
   consumer is the evidence-context assembly of [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context),
   and a read of it from any other decision site is a defect this packet's tests
   name rather than a stylistic preference.
5. **What the store published is what the decision pins.** The digest the store
   holds, its derivation version, its distance metric and its roots are the
   values that reach `evidence_basis` unchanged where the slot is `admitted`
   (C5, [W13](#el:w13-decision-trace)). A rebuild from the same ledger facts at
   the same envelope reproduces them byte-for-byte, which is what makes the
   pinned value checkable rather than merely recorded.
6. **The store answers only for what it holds.** A scope with no cell yields no
   Tier 1 for that candidate; no zero, no empty cell and no synthesized entry is
   returned in place of an absent one, so [W10](#el:w10-comparison).3's "absence
   is never read as no advantage" is not undone one layer down.
7. **Hermetic and deterministic.** No clock, no network and no accepted head is
   read; the boundary is a ledger position, and two runs over the same records
   write the same bytes.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` over: a fold–write–read round trip whose
read equals the fold that produced it; an advanced ledger causing a re-derivation
and an unmoved ledger not causing one; each of the four degraded read states
producing a usable **`enabled`** context under an enabled policy, with its exact
slot reason list, no refusal, and an emitted order equal to what the pinned
projection emits for that wake's applicable prior set — which is `base_order`
byte-for-byte in the sub-case where that set is empty and is **not** asserted to
be `base_order` in the sub-case where it is not; a tampered stored body reaching
the screens and being excluded there rather than trusted here; and a scope with
no cell producing no Tier 1. Mechanically
re-exercised on the delivered path by [W17](#el:w17-guard-fold),
[W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance), [W19](#el:w19-guard-prefer-only) and
[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades).

### W14 — The live objective binding seam in the Driver {#el:w14-driver-wiring}

*Discharges:* E2, C2, C29 (construction side), C34 (commit side).

**Seam.** The place a placement is actually made: the Driver's scheduler input
assembly and the `Bind` call it feeds. In the staged tree that path runs
`internal/cli/root.go` → `internal/driver/catalog.go` (policy load) →
`internal/driver/dispatch_recovery.go` / `internal/driver/session.go` (ready-set
and evaluation) → `internal/scheduler/scheduler.go` (`Bind`) and is recorded by
`internal/scheduler/evaluation.go`. Since [RPL-007](#el:revision-history) the seam has a
second half, in the same place: this is also where the wake's **evidence
context** is produced and handed to each placement. `internal/driver/placement.go`
is the file that holds both — it resolves the binding, reads the store of
[W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path) and the accepted observation snapshots
[EN13](#el:en13-live-prior-source) names, runs the screens of
[W8](#el:w8-snapshot-screen) and the assembly of [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context)
once, and constructs each placement call of that wake from the result. The
context has two evidence inputs and they degrade independently: the store
supplies the snapshot slot and the observations supply the priors, which is why
losing one leaves the other's rung standing.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The Driver resolves the objective from **one operative validated policy** and
   constructs the three declaration-derived arguments as images of that one
   binding. There is no code path that assembles them from two documents.
2. The requirement pin is taken from the pending Lane Requirement being placed,
   and `pass_id` from the pass it names — never chosen beside it.
3. All of this happens **before** `Bind`, so no binding is produced whose
   objective is unknown, retrospectively attributed, interpreted under an
   unrecorded projection, or assembled from unauthorized declarations.
4. The emitted order is what `Bind` consumes as its candidate order; every hard
   filter — capability, quality floor, independence exclusion, `max_lanes`
   saturation, effect class, repository policy — continues to run **upstream and
   unchanged**, and the existing refusal and deferral behaviour is preserved
   exactly.
5. When no validated policy is operative, or the call is unlawful, no binding is
   produced and `binding_unsatisfiable` surfaces through the existing
   vocabulary.
6. The decision is constructed against the observed head and committed by the
   conditional append of [EN6](#el:en6-append-seam).
7. `internal/scheduler/implementation.go`'s embedded source set and version are
   updated so the implementation pin follows the code the binary contains, as it
   does today.
8. **Every placement of a wake receives the assembled context.** Once per
   `Drive()` — [EN12](#el:en12-one-context-per-wake)'s unit — the Driver reads
   the current candidate snapshot from the store of
   [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path), runs the screens of
   [W8](#el:w8-snapshot-screen) and the assembly of
   [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context) **exactly once**, and supplies the resulting
   context to **every** placement call it makes in that wake. There is no path in
   the delivered tree by which a placement call is constructed with a context the
   assembly did not produce: no literal `disabled` value at a call site, no
   second per-call construction, and no caller-supplied override. This is the
   criterion the refused candidate lacked, and it is the one that makes the
   telemetry delivery reachable in production rather than merely present in it.
9. **`disabled` and `excluded` are decided, never defaulted, and they are decided
   by different things.** The context is `disabled` exactly where the one
   operative validated policy declares no `telemetry` block, and its slot is
   `excluded` exactly where the screens excluded the candidate the store returned
   or the store returned none — the four states of
   [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path).3, of which the last produces a reason list
   of exactly `[not_available]`. Neither value is ever reached because the
   assembly was skipped. Because [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) requires the
   delivered document to declare an enabled `telemetry` block, the delivered
   path's context is **`enabled` on every wake**, including a wake with no store
   at all; a delivery in which the live path can produce `disabled` while the
   operative document declares an enabled `telemetry` block fails this criterion
   regardless of what the rest of the machinery does, and so does one in which
   removing the store is what makes it `disabled`
   ([W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).8, [RPL-008](#el:revision)).
10. **The decision pins what the store published.** Every Lane Binding produced
    on this path records the assembled context's `context_digest`, and where the
    slot is `admitted`, the snapshot's digest, derivation version, distance
    metric and roots exactly as [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path) published them,
    in the `evidence_basis` members [W13](#el:w13-decision-trace) already
    declares. No trace shape changes here; what is new is that a produced
    decision carries live values rather than a canonical absence.
11. **The evidence path moves nothing upstream of it.** The store read and the
    assembly happen after every hard filter has produced the eligible set and
    before `order`. No filter outcome, refusal, deferral, escalation, gate
    result, claim, deadline or lane count is a function of the context, and the
    existing scheduler, evaluation, dispatch-recovery and placement-baseline
    suites stay green with no store present at all.
12. **The wake supplies the assembly its applicable prior set, and the ladder's
    first rung is therefore reachable.** The Driver reads the accepted
    model-scoped observation snapshots under `backends/observations/` through
    `internal/backend/observations` and hands the assembly the applicable prior
    list the pinned projection record's prior schema delivers over them, once per
    wake and beside the store read of .8, under
    [EN13](#el:en13-live-prior-source). `priors: []` is the **decided** empty
    state ([W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).4) — reached when no accepted
    observation applies to any candidate's declared model under the resolved
    objective, and reached in no other way. No policy state and no store state
    empties the list: a wake with no materialized store still carries its priors,
    so a delivery whose no-store wakes emit `base_order` while an applicable prior
    exists fails this criterion. This is the criterion the refused candidate
    lacked, and it is the one that makes `evidence-cold-degrades`'s first rung a
    state this repository can be in rather than a sentence about a function.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...` including the existing scheduler,
evaluation, dispatch-recovery and placement-baseline suites staying green, and
tests that:

- a policy that does not load yields no binding rather than a default order;
- **a wake with no materialized store records an `enabled` context whose slot is
  `excluded` with a reason list of exactly `[not_available]`, and never a
  `disabled` context**, since the delivered document declares an enabled
  `telemetry` block;
- **that same wake emits, for each placed pass, byte-for-byte what the pinned
  projection emits for `(objective.id, eligible, priors_in, base_order)`** with
  the wake's own applicable prior set — and emits `base_order` byte-for-byte,
  producing exactly today's bindings, **exactly in the sub-case where that set is
  empty**, with the emptiness established by the test's own observation input
  rather than assumed. Both sub-cases are exercised: one over an observation set
  supplying an applicable prior for a candidate's declared model, one over an
  observation set supplying none;
- in both sub-cases the eligible set, every hard-filter outcome, every refusal,
  deferral and escalation, every gate result, claim, deadline and the lane count
  are byte-for-byte those of the same wake run with the observation source
  removed as well, so the prior rung is prefer-only exactly as the snapshot rung
  is;
- two placements in one wake record one `context_digest` and one prior set; and
- a wake whose store holds an admitted snapshot records that snapshot's digest
  and derivation version in every binding it produces.

### W15 — The shared guard fixture corpus {#el:w15-fixture-corpus}

*Discharges:* C15's fixture-executability requirement; the corpus the five
guards range over.

**Seam.** A fixture tree beside the existing guard fixtures in `testdata/`,
following the pattern the present standing guards already use (a directory of
JSON cases loaded by a `go run`-able program that locates the tree root).

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The corpus supplies, entirely as data: policy documents, catalog contexts,
   backend contexts, Lane Requirements naming passes, a fixture ledger whose
   append positions and head are data, two fixture projections publishing
   **different** vocabularies, ledger fact sets including Lane Binding,
   submission and admission-outcome records, snapshots, evidence contexts and
   thresholds values in both variants, and candidate sets carrying their five
   declared values.
2. **No live infrastructure, no clock, no accepted-head dependence, no
   randomness.** Every marker, digest and pin is fixture data.
3. Fixtures exercise **boundaries, not interiors**: for every rule with a
   declared bound, the corpus holds the value at the bound and one past it. For
   the completion-mass equality that means the lawful cell at the equality and
   the two nearest unlawful ones — one sample's worth short and one sample's
   worth over — rather than a grossly malformed body, which would not
   distinguish the rule from its neighbours.
4. The zero-bound freshness fixtures are built as **production states** — the
   lag-0 case by folding the fixture ledger to the head the decision is then
   constructed against, and the lag-1 case by appending exactly one further
   record before that head is read — never by a marker no construction observed.
5. **The corpus carries the live-policy case.** For
   [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance)'s live-policy case it supplies: the two
   mutations of the operative document (`influence: none`, and the `telemetry`
   block removed) as data; the identification of one effort-sibling pair over
   one harness and one model that the live `backends/` declarations and the live
   preference lists already contain; and tuple-scoped outcome evidence for that
   pair at the live `min_samples` and at one sample below it, dominated on
   latency and adverse completion with no quality advantage. The operative
   document, the live catalog context and the live backend declarations
   themselves are **not copied into the corpus** — the guard reads them from the
   subject tree, which is the whole point of the case.
6. **Reading the operative document is not the accepted-head dependence (2)
   forbids.** Every registered guard identity declares `input_spec: subject-tree`
   and `cwd_spec: workspace`, so the repository's own files are part of the
   subject the check is defined over; the existing standing guards already read
   `policy/checks/repository.json` and
   `catalog/refusal-classes/refusal-classes.yaml` from the located tree root for
   exactly this reason, and `failure-diagnosis-shape` records the reason in its
   own comment — to bind the guard to the delivered enumeration rather than to a
   copy of it. The same distinction covers driving the delivered placement path
   under (8): the path is code in the subject tree, and the ledger it runs over
   is fixture data the guard itself writes. What (2) forbids is a dependence on an **accepted head in the
   graph store**, a clock, a network or live infrastructure: things that are not
   in the tree and can differ between two verification environments holding the
   same tree. A file in the subject tree is none of those, and the dual-signal
   argument in [Dual signal](#el:verification-dual-signal) is unaffected.
7. **The corpus carries the multi-rule case.** Fixture policy documents supply,
   entirely as data, the cases [F11](#el:f11-rule-selector) names:
   - a document with **two placement rules ordering the same backends
     differently**, so one backend id holds two different
     `declared_policy_position` values, and at least one **pair is inverted**
     between the rules — with the fixture declarations' ranks ordering that pair
     the *first* way, so a context that silently fell back to rank order would
     produce the first rule's answer for both passes and be caught. A fixture
     whose two rules differ only by a shift, never by an inversion, does not
     distinguish the rule-scoped position from the rank-scoped one and is not
     sufficient;
   - a rule matching a pass but declaring **no** `prefer.backends`, and a
     cataloged pass matched by **no** rule, so the empty-list degeneration to
     ascending `(declared_rank, backend_id)` is fixture data rather than an
     untested branch;
   - a rule whose `prefer.backends` **repeats** a backend id, for the preserved
     duplicate refusal of [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).7; and
   - a call carrying one rule's positions while placing a requirement whose pass
     matches the other, with `base_order` recomputed over those positions, for
     the wrong-rule unlawful case of [W12](#el:w12-call-coherence).2.

   For the **live** per-pass sweep the corpus supplies only the expected orders
   and the cataloged pass list; the operative document, the cataloged pass ids
   and the accepted declarations are read from the subject tree under (5) and
   (6), because a copied preference list is precisely the thing that could drift
   from the document the repository actually places by.
8. **The corpus carries the delivered-path case.** For the delivered-path clauses
   of [W16](#el:w16-guard-objective)–[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) it supplies,
   entirely as data: a ledger fact set a guard can materialize into a graph the
   Driver's own placement path runs over; the pending Lane Requirements that path
   places and the backend declarations it places them against; the tuple-scoped
   outcome facts that make one effort-sibling pair sufficiently sampled; and the
   three store states those clauses need — a store the delivered materialization
   produced, the same store with its body altered after publication, and no store
   at all.

   **It also supplies the two prior sets and the driven policy mutation that
   [RPL-008](#el:revision) requires**, because a delivered-path clause that cannot
   vary the prior set cannot tell the ladder's two rungs apart:
   - **An applicable model-scoped observation set** — accepted-shape snapshots
     under the [EN13](#el:en13-live-prior-source) reader's own format, supplying a
     prior for at least one placed candidate's declared model under the resolved
     objective, and ordering that candidate set **differently from `base_order`**,
     so the first rung is distinguishable from the floor rather than coinciding
     with it. A prior set whose projection output happens to equal `base_order`
     does not distinguish the rungs and is not sufficient, for the same reason
     (7)'s two-rule fixture must invert rather than shift.
   - **A set under which no prior applies** — the empty snapshot and,
     independently, a snapshot whose subjects name no declared model — so the
     `base_order` floor is reached with its precondition established rather than
     assumed.
   - **The telemetry-block-removed mutation of the operative document as a driven
     input**, not only as the pure-seam mutation (5) already supplies, so the
     `disabled` context can be reached on the delivered path by the one thing
     that produces it and the guard can show that it too keeps the prior rung.

   **Materializing a fixture graph is not the live-infrastructure dependence (2)
   forbids, and the distinction is the same one (6) draws.** What (2) rules out
   is a dependence on something that can differ between two verification
   environments holding the same tree: a clock, a network, an accepted head in a
   graph store the guard did not build. A graph the guard writes from fixture
   records into a directory it creates and removes is none of those — it is
   fixture data materialized to disk, which is the only form in which the
   delivered path can consume it. Every value a guard asserts on remains either
   fixture data or the two sides of an equality computed within the same run
   (a pinned digest and its rebuild, an evidence-on order and an evidence-off
   order), so the verdict stays a function of the tree alone. Driving the
   delivered path rather than calling `order` is precisely what
   [RPL-007](#el:revision-history) found missing: a guard that only calls the pure
   function cannot see whether anything calls it.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...`. The corpus's real teeth are the five
guards that consume it.

### W18 — `guard-dominance-demotes` {#el:w18-guard-dominance}

*Discharges:* evaluator node `guard-dominance-demotes`, check id
`de7aec13ddb58d8ab2cad6a12e7aa396db261d1c2ed6c3dc495eaabadaff6192`; the
operative half of `unearned-effort-demotes` over the delivered policy.

**Seam.** The program `tools/standing-guards/placement-dominance-demotes`.

**Acceptance criteria.** A satisfying fixture demotes, `final_rank` reflects it,
and the trace names the pair with its compared statistics, per-dimension counts
and thresholds; every named non-demotion condition holds, including each margin
boundary exactly; declaration-digest mismatch suppressing the demotion with
`tier1_blockers: [declaration_mismatch]` and its restoration; an undeclared
effort coordinate producing **no Lane Binding at all** rather than a different
order; derivation mismatch suppressing the demotion while the bind occurs;
multi-lane evidence reaching `min_samples` only at binding grain, with the same
fixture at pass-run grain demoting nothing; the intervening-candidate extension
emitting exactly `[X, lower, higher]` and two overlapping demotions emitting the
lexicographically least extension; an already-satisfied nonempty constraint set
emitting the `prefer` order unchanged **and** recording every pair; pass scoping
demoting under one pass and not the other over an otherwise identical call; a
later bind marker removing the demotion with `tier1_blockers:
[snapshot_not_fresh]`; unmeasured dimensions yielding no demotion because
they are not determinable rather than because an omitted value was read; and
**the deficient-mass pair**, in which one fixture publishes a higher-effort cell
whose completion mass is short by the adverse observations that would have
carried the demotion — the snapshot is excluded, `tier1_blockers` is exactly
`[snapshot_excluded]`, the bind still happens, and **no demotion verdict of
either sign rides on that cell** — while the same fixture at full mass demotes
with its pair recorded, so the guard distinguishes "the evidence says do not
demote" from "the evidence was never complete enough to say".

**And the live-policy case, which is not a fixture case.** Every clause above
ranges over the fixture corpus, and a guard that ranges only over fixtures
accepts a delivery whose operative policy has tuple evidence switched off — or,
as [RPL-007](#el:revision-history) found, one whose policy declares it on and whose
Driver never asks for it. So this guard additionally binds to the **delivered**
document and the **delivered placement path**, reading `policy/scheduler.yaml`,
the cataloged pass ids and the accepted declarations under `backends/` from the
subject tree the registered check already runs over
([W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).5–.6) and driving the Driver's own placement path
over a fixture ledger it materializes ([W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8). It
establishes all of the following:

- **The live document is operative and enabled.** It loads against the live
  catalog context with a clean verdict, its `telemetry.influence` is
  `prefer_only`, and `thresholds_of` resolves it to the **`enabled`** variant.
  The guard fails on `disabled`, on a refused load, and on any other `influence`
  value — so it fails on the exact state RPL-003 described.
- **The demotion is the delivered path's, not a constructed call's.** The
  demoting run is produced by driving the Driver's placement path, so the
  snapshot the demotion rides on is the one
  [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path)'s materialization published, the context is
  the one [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).8 assembled for that wake, and the
  recorded `evidence_basis` carries that store's digest and derivation version.
  The guard **fails** if the demotion is reachable only by calling `order` with a
  hand-built context, and it fails if removing the materialized store leaves the
  demotion standing — the first because it is the refused candidate's exact
  state, the second because a demotion that survives its own evidence is not
  riding on it.
- **A sufficiently sampled dominated effort sibling actually demotes under it.**
  Over the live policy, live catalog context and live backend declarations, with
  the corpus's tuple-scoped evidence for one declared effort-sibling pair of one
  harness and one model at or above the live `min_samples`, the higher-effort
  tuple is ordered **after** its lower-effort sibling for that pass, the pair
  appears in `demotions` with its compared statistics, per-dimension counts and
  the live thresholds, and `final_rank` reflects it. The staged tree already
  holds a witness: `claude-opus-4-8-high` and `claude-opus-4-8-medium` are
  accepted declarations over one harness (`runtime_id: claude-code`) and one
  model, both `supported_pass_types: [build]`, with the higher-effort tuple
  ordered **before** the lower in the live `build-single-agent-coding` preference
  list — so the demotion displaces rather than merely being recorded, and the
  case cannot pass by accident. The positions are read under the rule the
  placed pass resolves to — for a `build` requirement that is
  `build-single-agent-coding` — and the guard asserts the recorded
  `matched_placement_rule` **is** that rule rather than assuming it, since a
  position read from another rule's list orders the same candidates differently
  ([F11](#el:f11-rule-selector)). The pair is **selected from the live
  declarations and preference lists**, not hard-coded: the guard names the
  witness only to prove the case is reachable in the staged tree, and it fails —
  rather than silently skipping — if the delivered document contains no
  effort-sibling pair over one harness and one model that is eligible for a
  common pass. A guard that skips when it cannot find its subject is a guard
  that accepts a policy edit which removes the property.
- **Nothing else moves.** The evidence-off comparison run is the same live wake
  with the materialized store removed — the store removed, never an argument
  substituted, since a substituted argument is the refused candidate's construction
  — and it differs from the demoting run in candidate **order alone**: the
  eligible set, the candidate multiset, every hard-filter outcome (capability,
  quality floor, independence exclusion, `max_lanes` saturation, effect class,
  repository policy), every gate result, every claim, every deadline and the
  lane count are byte-for-byte equal. This is `telemetry-is-prefer-only` checked
  on the live document rather than on a fixture.

  **That run's context is `enabled`, not `disabled`, and the guard asserts it.**
  The delivered document declares an enabled `telemetry` block
  ([W4](#el:w4-policy-document)), so removing its store leaves an `enabled`
  context whose snapshot slot is `excluded` with a reason list of exactly
  `[not_available]`, `tier1_blockers` exactly `[snapshot_excluded]`, and
  `thresholds_variant` still `enabled`. The guard **fails** on a run that records
  `context_variant: disabled` here, because that trace states that the operative
  document declares no telemetry when it declares some
  ([W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).8, [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).9). The
  comparison also leaves the prior rung standing on both sides: the two runs
  carry the same applicable prior set, so the order difference the guard measures
  is the demotion and nothing else.
- **The case cannot be satisfied by a fixture policy.** The guard fails if the
  demotion it reports is reachable only from a corpus policy — asserted by
  driving the same evidence through the delivered path under the live document
  with its `telemetry` block **removed**, where the demotion must **not** occur,
  `thresholds_variant` and `context_variant` must **both** be `disabled`, and the
  emitted order must still be what the pinned projection emits for that wake's
  applicable prior set, since telemetry-block absence disables Tier 1 alone and
  never the prior rung ([W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).8). That mutation is the one
  input that produces a `disabled` context anywhere on this path, which is what
  makes it the discriminator. A guard that cannot tell the two apart is not
  checking the delivery.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...`. Then executed as its registered
identity at [W21](#el:w21-delivery).

**Acceptance criteria.** With no Tier 1 evidence anywhere, on each of the listed

`base_order` values, each byte-for-byte the pre-@3 order for its own pass, with
the inverted pair inverted; the same set placed under the pass matched by the
rule declaring no `prefer.backends`, and under the pass matched by no rule at
all, emits ascending `(declared_rank, backend_id)`, which for the inverted pair

fell back to ranks would be caught; and over the **live** document the guard
sweeps **every** cataloged pass id under `catalog/passes/`, asserting for each

telemetry-block absence does not
bypass the prior rung; a projection mismatch yields
`tier2_blockers: [projection_mismatch]`, ignores the carried priors entirely and
emits `base_order`; the `disabled` variant is exercised as its **exact
serialized state**, with two builders agreeing on `context_digest` and an
invented metric, bound or verdict failing; **both mixed calls** are exercised end
to end with their exact traces, the `admitted`-slot-under-`disabled`-thresholds
call recording no `freshness` block, no `thresholds` block and exactly
`[thresholds_disabled]`, and the thresholds-tag toggle over one otherwise
identical call making the `freshness` block appear and disappear with that tag
alone; every freshness boundary is reached by varying `bind_marker`, with the
zero-bound cases built as production states and the `ill_ordered` case exercised
only as a hand-recorded trace; one unchanged evidence context crosses the
boundary as the marker advances; per-dimension tiering is recorded as such;
channel separation under partial mixed-dimension evidence fixes one expected
`prefer_rank` vector and one `final_rank` vector, with `prefer_rank`
byte-for-byte unchanged when only Tier 1 statistics vary; the determined base
order rejects both a differing supplied order and a changed rank with a
correspondingly recomputed order; an accounting entry supplies no tier,
statistic, demotion or position; and replay reproduces the recorded order, lag
and verdict, with the adjacency equality checked and marker equality failing.

**And the delivered path degrades down the same two rungs.** This clause is
[RPL-008](#el:revision)'s disposition and it replaces a weaker one that asserted
a single unconditional floor over a mis-stated context variant. Over a fixture
ledger materialized under [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8, the guard drives the
**Driver's own placement path** in **three** wakes and asserts, in each, both the
recorded state and both rungs.

The three wakes, and the state each must record:

| Wake | `context_variant` | Snapshot slot | Tier 1 blockers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delivered document, **no materialized store** | `enabled` | `excluded`, reasons exactly `[not_available]`, no candidate digest, no observed envelope | `[snapshot_excluded]` |
| Delivered document, **store the screens exclude** | `enabled` | `excluded`, its exact ordered reason list with the retained candidate digest and observed envelope | `[snapshot_excluded]` |
| **Telemetry-block-removed** mutation of that document | `disabled`, in its exact four-member serialization | absent — the variant has no slot | `[context_disabled, thresholds_disabled]` |

The guard **fails** on any wake of the first two that records
`context_variant: disabled`: the delivered document declares an enabled
`telemetry` block ([W4](#el:w4-policy-document)), so a trace saying otherwise is a
false statement about the operative policy, and a delivery in which removing a
store is what disables telemetry has conflated the two axes
[W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).8 separates. The third wake is where `disabled`
legitimately appears, and it appears there because the policy says so.

And in **each** of the three, the two rungs are asserted separately, because a
single assertion cannot distinguish them:

- **First rung.** Run over
  [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8's applicable observation set, the emitted order
  for each placed pass is byte-for-byte what the **pinned projection** emits for
  `(objective.id, eligible, priors_in, base_order)` with that wake's prior set,
  the constraint set is empty and `refine` is the identity. The corpus's
  applicable set orders its candidates differently from `base_order`, so this
  assertion **fails** against a delivery that dropped the prior rung instead of
  passing by coincidence.
- **Second rung.** Run over
  [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8's no-applicable-prior sets — the empty snapshot
  and, independently, one naming no declared model — `priors` is `[]`, and only
  then is the emitted order `base_order` byte-for-byte, with the wake producing
  the same bindings, refusals and deferrals as the pre-@3 path over the same
  fixture ledger. The same equality is reached a third way, by a projection-pin
  mismatch yielding `tier2_blockers: [projection_mismatch]` with a nonempty
  carried prior list ignored entirely, which is the design's other route to an
  empty `priors_in`.

A guard that asserts the `base_order` equality without establishing that the
applicable prior set is empty is checking a coincidence, not the floor; and one
that establishes the cold-start equalities only over constructed calls does not
establish that the repository's own placements degrade at all, because on that
path the context is a value the caller chose.

**Verification.** `code`; `go test ./...`. Then executed as its registered
identity at [W21](#el:w21-delivery).

### W21 — Delivery: documentation, derivation basis, and the evaluator check sets {#el:w21-delivery}

*Discharges:* C14 (the basis the observation pass admits), C15, D0001.C13
admission completeness. *This is the delivery packet the target state requires
to name `placement-outcome-guards` and `stalls-guards`.*

**Seam.** The canonical Diátaxis documentation for telemetry-informed placement
and the fold's derivation basis, written into the existing docs tree and the
RFC 0008 implementation note.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. `docs/reference/scheduler-policy.md` documents the `placement_objectives`
   and `telemetry` shapes, the closed refusal set, the declared value domains,
   and the resolved policy binding — including the `influence` declaration, its
   single admissible value `prefer_only`, the fact that it is optional and is
   decided at load rather than carried at the seam
   ([EN8](#el:en8-influence-declaration)), and the fact that the operative
   document declares it. The design's own off switch — an absent `telemetry`
   block — is documented beside it, so a reader looking for "how do I turn this
   off" finds the answer that exists rather than the one the old `influence:
   none` line implied. It **also** documents the matched-rule resolution
   ([EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition)): that
   `declared_policy_position` is an index into one rule's `prefer.backends`
   list, that the rule is the first in declared order whose `match.pass` names
   the placed pass, that a pass matching no such rule falls to the declaration
   rank, that only `match.pass` is read today, and that the same backend can
   therefore hold different positions under different passes — with the live
   inverted pair named as the worked example, because a reader editing one
   preference list needs to know it is editing one pass's floor and not the
   fleet's.
2. `docs/reference/scheduler-telemetry-fold.md` is the **derivation basis** the
   `observation` pass admits as an Exogenous Change Record: the fold
   specification, its inclusion rule and structural exclusion, its window and
   distance metric, its accounting partition, **its completion partition — the
   four categories, the total assignment of
   [EN7](#el:en7-completion-category) that produces them, and the equality that
   closes them over `samples`** — its three root families, its envelope/body
   split and its derivation version. The completion partition belongs in the
   basis and not only in the code because a rebuilder that assigns a category
   differently is a different fold and cannot reproduce the digest, so a basis
   that omits the assignment does not state what it takes to rebuild. It
   documents the *basis*, not a snapshot; the snapshot stays a derived store and
   is never admitted as an artifact.
3. `docs/explanation/telemetry-informed-placement.md` explains why merit is
   scoped to `(backend_id, pass_id)`, why influence is prefer-only, and what
   cold start guarantees; `docs/concepts/scheduler.md` is updated where it now
   understates the seam.
4. `rfcs/0008-scheduler-and-capacity-policy/implementation.md` records the v3
   implementation as the RFC's own lineage entry.
5. The three index files are updated so every new document is reachable.
6. Implementation never outruns explanation: this packet lands with the code, in
   the same delivery, which is what P11 requires.
7. **The live evidence path is documented where a reader can act on it.**
   `docs/reference/scheduler-telemetry-fold.md` also states where this repository
   materializes the snapshot, when it is re-derived, what a reader sees in each
   of the four states a read can return, that one evidence context is assembled
   per wake and serves every placement of that wake, and that the digest the
   store published is the digest the decision pins. A basis that says how to
   rebuild the fold but not where the repository keeps it does not let a reader
   check the pin they are holding, and the failure
   [RPL-007](#el:revision-history) found is invisible to a reader who cannot ask that
   question. `docs/explanation/telemetry-informed-placement.md` carries the same
   fact in its own register: what a placement does when there is no store yet,
   which is the ordinary state of a repository that has just adopted this.
8. **Both cold-start rungs are documented, with the source of each.**
   `docs/reference/scheduler-policy.md` states where a live wake's applicable
   priors come from — the accepted model-scoped observation snapshots under
   `backends/observations/`, read through the pinned projection record's prior
   schema ([EN13](#el:en13-live-prior-source)) — and that an absent or empty
   snapshot is the `priors: []` state rather than an error.
   `docs/explanation/telemetry-informed-placement.md` states the ladder in the
   order the predicate states it: tuple evidence first, then the applicable model
   prior, then the declared policy/rank/id order, with the note that a wake
   missing its telemetry store is on the **middle** rung and not the floor, and
   that only a wake with no applicable prior either emits the declared order.
   Without this a reader who removes the store and sees the order change has no
   way to tell a working degradation from a broken one — which is the question
   [RPL-008](#el:revision) is about.

**Verification.** `placement-outcome-guards` — all five registered guard
identities, now executable, must accept; `stalls-guards` — the five existing
standing guards must continue to accept; and `code`.

### Dual signal {#el:verification-dual-signal}

The evaluator's `signal_rule` requires two independent, mechanically applicable,
admitted reports over the exact integrated Product tree, **with the placement
guards executing inside both**. The guards satisfy the applicability half by
construction and by [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus)'s acceptance criteria: each is
a `go run`-able program over the subject tree with no live infrastructure, no
clock, no network and no accepted-head dependence, so it executes identically in
any verification environment that has the tree. `network: false` is already
declared on all five registered identities, and every one declares
`input_spec: subject-tree` with `cwd_spec: workspace`.

Three properties are worth calling out because a plan that assumed them would be
wrong:

- **The guards must run in both signals, not merely be runnable.** Producing the
  two reports is the `verification` pass's work over the integrated head, not a
  packet here. What this plan owes it is guards that *can* run in both, and
  [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus) is where that is decided.
- **The zero-bound fixtures must be production states.** A guard whose lag-0
  case is reachable only by giving a snapshot a high-water mark equal to the
  carrying record's own position exercises the pure function and not the path a
  repository takes. [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).4 and
  [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) both refuse that construction.
- **Driving the delivered path is applicability too, and it is what
  [RPL-007](#el:revision-history) requires.** Four of the five guards now establish a
  clause by running the Driver's own placement path over a graph they materialize
  from fixture records in a directory they create and remove
  ([W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8). That adds no clock, no network, no accepted
  head and no live infrastructure: the code is the subject tree's, the records
  are fixture data, and the directory is the guard's own. Two verification
  environments holding the same tree therefore reach the same verdict, which is
  what mechanical applicability means. A guard that can only call `order` is
  fixture-executable and blind to whether anything calls it — which is precisely
  the delivery the refused candidate permitted.
- **Reading the delivered tree is applicability, not a dependence.**
  [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance)'s live-policy case reads
  `policy/scheduler.yaml`, the cataloged pass ids and the accepted `backends/`
  declarations from the located tree root. Those files are inside
  `input_spec: subject-tree`, so the case is a function of the exact integrated
  Product tree and of nothing else — which is what mechanical applicability
  means, and is the same thing the existing `lint-rfcs-verifier-bidirectional`
  and `failure-diagnosis-shape` guards already do with
  `policy/checks/repository.json` and the refusal-class catalog. Two
  verification environments holding the same tree therefore reach the same
  verdict, and the case adds no clock, no network, no accepted head and no live
  infrastructure. [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).6 states the distinction where
  the no-dependence rule is declared.

### Per-packet mechanical teeth {#el:verification-teeth}

Every code and data packet gates on the registered `code` set — `go build ./...`,
`go vet ./...`, `go run ./tools/lint-rfcs`, `go run ./tools/gofmtcheck` — **and**
on the registered `go test ./...` identity
`62d3fbf53edbf60366f80ec277fba345c3c48fb24c6d4224dbc3862cb05ea9c0`, so the unit
tests each packet's acceptance criteria name are teeth rather than intentions.
`code` alone would let a packet land with a failing suite, because `go test` is
not a member of it.

`lint-rfcs` is load-bearing for two of this delivery's obligations and is
therefore in every gate: it enforces that no retired term reaches
`internal/`, `cmd/`, `tools/`, `catalog/` or `policy/`, and that no model name
appears outside `backends/` — which is precisely why
[W5](#el:w5-backend-context) declares the coordinates in `backends/` and nowhere
else.

### Work-graph dependency closure {#el:verification-closure}

A packet boundary is only verifiable if the packet can actually be built when it
is reached. This subsection states that property as a rule, and records the
result of checking it over every packet — the disposition RPL-001 asked for and
the one [RPL-005](#el:revision) found violated in its stronger form.

**The rule.** For every packet `P` and every path `F` in `P.inputs`, if some
sibling `Q` declares `F` among its outputs, then `P` and `Q` must be **ordered**
by `depends_on`, in one of exactly two ways:

- **Forward (`Q` before `P`).** `P` reads what `Q` produced, so `Q` is in `P`'s
  transitive `depends_on` closure. This is the normal case.
- **Base-state (`P` before `Q`).** `P` reads the file as it stands in the staged
  tree and `Q` later rewrites it, so `P` is in `Q`'s transitive closure. `P` is
  a strict ancestor of `Q`, which means **every** lawful topological execution
  runs `P` first; `P` can never observe `Q`'s edit.

What is forbidden is the third case: `P` and `Q` unordered, neither reaching the
other. Then the execution order decides what `P` reads, and the graph does not
determine the build.

**The rule is necessary and not sufficient, which is what
[RPL-005](#el:revision) established.** The prior candidate satisfied it exactly:
`p04` read `policy/scheduler.yaml` as a *legal base-state read*, and the closure
table said so in as many words. The read was ordered and still wrong, because a
base-state read is only sound when the staged state is a state the reader can
**use**. `policy/scheduler.yaml` in the staged tree refuses to load under the
validator that packet depends on, so what the reader would have observed was not
a weaker input but no input at all. So the rule gains a second clause, and it is
the clause this revision is about:

- **Usability.** A base-state read is legal only if the file *as staged*
  satisfies whatever the reader's own acceptance criteria demand of it. Where a
  packet's criteria require the file to be **valid under something this delivery
  introduces**, the staged bytes are by construction not that, and the read must
  be forward — the packet must sit after whatever makes them valid.

The distinction is mechanical to apply: read the reader's acceptance criteria and
ask whether they quantify over a property the staged file has. `p02` reads
`internal/scheduler/scheduler.go` for a representation that exists today: legal.
`p05` reads `policy/scheduler.yaml` for a *resolved policy binding*, which the
staged document cannot produce: not legal, and now forward.

**The result.** Re-derived exhaustively from the block below — not carried over:
**102** produced-input reads across the twenty-two packets, of which **93 are
forward** and **9 are base-state**, and **none is unordered**. The relation is
computed at **path-prefix grain**: an input is a produced-input read of a sibling
whose declared output is that path or a directory containing it, which is what
makes `p14`'s read of `backends/observations` a read of `p05`'s `backends`
output rather than an unproduced staged-tree read. Every base-state
row was additionally checked against the usability clause. The write scopes of
every pair of mutually unordered packets are pairwise disjoint, every packet's
outputs lie inside its own write scope, no path is an output of two packets, the
graph is acyclic, and the authored index is a topological order of it.

[RPL-007](#el:revision-history) added twenty of those reads and **no** base-state row.
`p22`'s three produced-input reads are forward by its two direct edges — `p07`

The seventeen delivered-path reads `p14`–`p21` gained are forward for the same

`p15`–`p21` sit after `p14`, so `internal/driver/telemetry_store.go` and

reads of code, schema and text whose staged form is exactly the thing being read;
none of them is a read of something this delivery first has to make valid.

One consequence is worth stating because it looks like an omission: no packet
declares an edge *to* a packet that later rewrites a file it read. Such an edge
would be a cycle in **every** row above — the reader is already an ancestor of
the writer, which is precisely what makes the row base-state rather than
unordered — and it would be the wrong claim anyway: the reader does not want the
rewritten file, it wants the one in the staged tree. The ordering that protects
it is the writer's own dependence on the reader, which is already in the graph.

**Where this is checked.** The property is a fact about the block below and is
decided by reading it: outputs, inputs and `depends_on` are all explicit, so the
closure is computable from the work graph alone with no repository state. The
usability clause is not computable that way — it is a statement about acceptance
criteria — so it is checked here by reading each base-state row's criteria
against the staged file, and the table records the result per row rather than as
a summary. Both are re-checkable by any reviewer. Mechanizing the first inside
`internal/workgraph`'s legality predicate would be a change to the compiler's own
L4 arithmetic, which no element of this design authorizes; it is recorded in
[Deferrals](#el:deferrals) rather than smuggled into a packet here.

## Deferrals {#el:deferrals}

Stated honestly, with what each would take.

1. **The merit Exogenous Change Record is not build work.**
   [W21](#el:w21-delivery) authors the derivation basis as a Product Artifact;
   admitting it as an ECR is a run of the `observation` pass against the
   integrated head, and the `merit-ecr-admitted` node accepts only after that
   run. No packet can perform it, because a build packet produces a Change Set,
   not admitted evidence.
2. **The D0008 Decision Record is regenerated by `decision-generation`.**
   D0005.C7 puts decision-record regeneration on an accepted-stage transition,
   not in a build packet. `decisions/D0008-scheduler-and-capacity-policy.md` is
   therefore untouched here.
3. **Two sibling guard sets stay unbacked** — see
   [F8](#el:f8-sibling-guard-sets). `placement-guards` still lacks
   `placement-prior-stays-model-scoped` and
   `placement-objectives-stay-independent`; `stalls5-guards` lacks all four of
   its entrypoints. Neither is an evaluator node of this target state. Closing
   them is a separate delivery against their own target states.
4. **`policy:repository` remains a placeholder.** Where this plan says
   "repository policy" as a hard filter the ordering never disturbs, it binds to
   whatever that policy becomes. A later pass must reconcile against the real
   policy once its owning RFC is accepted.
5. **Token-cost dominance is not delivered and no proxy is substituted.**
   `cost_observed` folds only what the ledger actually reports as a cost
   attribution, in the unit it reports; wall-clock and failure shape are never
   relabelled as token facts. Direct token-cost dominance requires its own
   record.
6. **The pass-contract environment amendment is not attempted.** Taking the
   projection record as a design-level `constrained_by` pin would require the
   `design-convergence` contract's `environment` block to declare it first;
   [W1](#el:w1-projection-record) discharges the dependence through the pin and
   the P-A–P-D contract instead, exactly as the design does.
7. **The produced-input closure rule is not mechanized in the legality
   predicate.** `internal/workgraph`'s `Legal` today enforces acyclicity, a
   plan-element anchor per packet, disjoint write scopes among parallel packets
   and a topological authored index — it does not compare `inputs` against
   sibling `outputs`, which is why RPL-001 could reach a review at all. Adding
   that rule would change the compiler's own L4 arithmetic and would touch a
   package no element of this design describes, so it is not delivery work here
   and no packet claims it. This plan discharges the obligation the way an L3
   artifact can: the property is stated as a rule, checked exhaustively over
   this graph in [Work-graph dependency closure](#el:verification-closure), and
   left re-checkable from the block's own three fields. Mechanizing it belongs
   to a delivery against the work-graph legality target state, where it would
   need its own guard and its own registered check. The rule's **usability**
   clause, which [RPL-005](#el:revision) added, is not mechanizable there at all
   and is not deferred to it: it quantifies over what a packet's acceptance
   criteria demand of a file, which is prose in this artifact and not a field of
   the block. It is checked here, per base-state row, in the closure table's
   fourth column, and it stays a reviewer's obligation rather than a
   compiler's.
8. **The materialized telemetry store is re-derived, never incrementally
   maintained.** [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path).2 refreshes by folding the
   window again when the ledger has advanced; it does not apply a delta to the
   stored snapshot. That is the honest cost of the rebuild equality
   [W17](#el:w17-guard-fold) checks — an incremental update is a second producer
   of the same digest, and a second producer is exactly what
   [W8](#el:w8-snapshot-screen)'s derivation-agreement screen exists to catch.
   Making the maintenance incremental would need its own derivation-version bump,
   its own equality between the delta path and the full fold, and a fixture pair
   for every category the delta can move; it is a strengthening against the same
   target state rather than work this delivery withholds. The Driver's existing
   fold snapshot shows the shape a later delivery would follow.
9. **Richer `placement_rules[].match` qualifiers are not lowered.** Under
   [EN3](#el:en3-backend-context-composition) the matched-rule resolution reads
   `match.pass` only, exactly as `ParseSchedulerBackendPreferences` does today.
   Two live rules carry more — `gating-review-independent`
   (`{pass: review, gating: true}`) and `harvest-expiring-first`
   (`{mode: harvest}`) — and neither qualifier decides anything today: the first
   declares no `prefer.backends` and is in any case preceded by
   `review-timely-first` for its pass, and the second names no pass and so
   contributes to no preference at all. Neither qualifier can change a floor
   today, which is what makes the restriction safe now and a real decision
   later. Lowering them would change which list a
   pass matches, which is re-tuning placement rather than encoding it, and it
   would break the pre-@3 equality [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).4 is stated as.
   Making `gating` and `mode` load-bearing selectors is its own delivery, and it
   would need a decision about what a gating review's declared floor should be —
   an L2 question this plan has no authority over.
   [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).6 asserts the restriction rather than leaving it
   implicit, so a later delivery that lowers the qualifiers will fail a check
   rather than silently change a floor.

## Risks and sequencing notes {#el:risks}

- **The policy document must precede everything that reads a validated policy,
  not merely the wiring.** [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) is a dependency of
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context) and of [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring) for the reason
  [F5](#el:f5-incomplete-telemetry) gives and
  [EN10](#el:en10-document-before-surface) generalizes: the staged document does
  not load under the new validator, so before that packet there is no resolved
  binding, no objective, no thresholds and no exposed rule surface anywhere in
  this repository. `code` alone would not catch a violation, because
  `go build`/`go vet` do not read policy. The `go test ./...` gate and the
  existing placement-baseline suite are what turn the ordering into a checked
  property rather than a hope.
- **A refused document is not a degraded input, and that is the trap
  [RPL-005](#el:revision) caught.** The failure mode reads as an ordering
  preference and is not one. Every other base-state read in this graph observes
  a *weaker but usable* staged state — a schema that constrains nothing, a
  scheduler that orders without evidence, a reader that parses fewer keys — and
  a packet built against it is correct as far as it goes. A refused policy
  document is categorically different: it yields **nothing**, so a packet whose
  API takes a validated policy has no input at all, and the only ways to make it
  compile are to bypass the validator or to fabricate the successor's document.
  Both defeat the single-validator seam. The tell is textual and worth looking
  for elsewhere: a packet whose acceptance criteria quantify over a property
  *this delivery introduces* — "validated", "resolved", "admitted", "loads" —
  cannot read its subject base-state. That is the usability clause in
  [Work-graph dependency closure](#el:verification-closure), and it is the one
  rule in this plan that a legal-looking dependency graph does not enforce.
- **Reordering the two packets moves what the equalities are stated over, and
  that has to stay checked from both sides.** With
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context) now reading the delivered document, nothing in the
  graph *structurally* prevents [W4](#el:w4-policy-document) from re-tuning a
  preference list and thereby quietly changing the floor that
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).4 then asserts as "the pre-@3 order". The
  disposition is that the preservation is checked rather than promised:
  [W4](#el:w4-policy-document).7 states it, .8 asserts it three ways over the
  delivered bytes — the five/seven projection-shape partition, the unedited
  placement-baseline fixture, and the top-level placement of the new blocks —
  and [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).6 re-derives the same partition from the other
  side of the seam. A future editor who re-tunes placement in this packet fails
  a check rather than moves a baseline.
- **A correct mechanism over an inoperative document is the failure mode with
  no symptom.** Every other risk in this list announces itself: a build cycles,
  a digest differs, a call is unlawful, a policy refuses. A delivery whose code
  is entirely correct and whose operative policy still declares tuple evidence
  off compiles, passes `go test ./...`, passes all five guards over their
  fixture corpora, and simply never demotes anything — the merit machinery runs
  and influences nothing. That is why the disposition is three-layered and no
  layer is redundant: [W3](#el:w3-policy-validator).10 makes the disabling value
  refuse the load, so the state is unreachable rather than merely undesirable;
  [W4](#el:w4-policy-document).5–.6 assert the live resolution and its two
  discriminating mutations, so the assertion is about this document; and
  [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance)'s live-policy case demotes over the delivered
  document, so the evaluator node that covers `unearned-effort-demotes` is
  measuring the repository rather than a fixture. Any one alone leaves the mode
  open — the validator alone cannot see a document nobody loads in a test, the
  test alone does not prove a demotion follows, and the guard alone would pass
  on a fixture policy.
- **[W5](#el:w5-backend-context) is the widest blast radius.** It touches every
  accepted declaration under `backends/`. Its acceptance criterion (2) — that
  declared coordinates must agree with the adapter command already pinned — is
  what keeps a sixty-file edit from silently introducing a coordinate no backend
  actually runs at. Criterion (4)'s restriction is the same concern one level
  down: the packet's write scope covers `backends`, which contains the recorded
  placement baseline it is measured against, so the fixture under
  `backends/testdata/` is declared off limits rather than merely left alone.
- **A single-valued policy position is the failure mode that looks correct.**
  This is [RPL-004](#el:revision)'s shape and it is worth stating as a standing
  risk, because the wrong implementation passes most tests. A backend context
  that yields one position per backend — from the first matching rule, or from
  the longest list, or from a merged union — compiles, is total, is
  rebuildable, is content-addressable, and produces the *correct* order for
  every pass whose candidates happen to be listed in only one rule. It goes
  wrong only where a candidate set spans two lists, and then it goes wrong
  silently: no refusal, no digest mismatch, no unlawful call, just a different
  cold-start floor than the one the repository declares. Nine live backend ids
  are in that overlap and one live pair inverts across it. The disposition is
  three-layered for the same reason the `influence` one is:
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).3 makes the selector an argument so the
  single-valued context is unconstructible rather than merely wrong;
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context).6 anchors the resolution to the function the
  repository already places by, so it cannot drift from it; and
  [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) sweeps every cataloged pass over the delivered
  document, so the check is over the repository rather than over a fixture.
  Fixtures that differ between rules only by a *shift* do not catch it —
  [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).7 requires an inversion, and requires the
  fixture ranks to order that pair the other way, precisely so that a rank
  fallback is distinguishable from a rule read.
- **Correct machinery nobody calls is the failure mode with no symptom at
  all**, and it is the one [RPL-007](#el:revision-history) caught. It is worse than the
  inoperative-document mode above, because that one at least leaves a value in a
  file a reader can look at: here the policy declares telemetry on, the fold is
  correct, the screens are correct, the seam is correct, every unit test passes,
  all five guards pass over their corpora, and no placement in the repository has
  ever seen an evidence context. Nothing is wrong; nothing happens. The
  disposition is three-layered for the same reason the other two are, and the
  layers are deliberately of different kinds: [W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path)
  makes the evidence *exist* in a running Driver, so the state "there is nothing
  to supply" is reachable only when the ledger genuinely holds nothing;
  [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).8–.9 makes the supply *universal and undefaultable*
  over a wake, so "the Driver did not ask" is unconstructible rather than merely
  undesirable; and the delivered-path clauses of
  [W17](#el:w17-guard-fold)–[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) make a **registered**
  guard drive the path a wake drives, so the property is measured where the
  evaluator looks. Any one alone leaves the mode open: the store alone is a file
  nobody reads, the criterion alone is a sentence in a plan, and a guard over
  constructed calls is exactly what passed while the mode was live.
- **A cold order that is right for the wrong reason is the failure mode that
  passes its own guard**, and it is the one [RPL-008](#el:revision) caught. The
  two cold-start rungs coincide exactly when no applicable prior exists — and a
  fixture with no observation set is the easiest fixture to write, so the
  coincidence is the *default* state of a carelessly built corpus. A delivery
  that never consults priors at all then passes a `base_order` assertion on every
  cold wake, and the guard reports that the repository degrades to its declared
  floor when what it actually does is have no ladder. The tell is textual and
  worth looking for in review: a cold-start assertion that names `base_order`
  without naming the prior set it holds under is not checking the floor. The
  disposition is three-layered like the others and the layers are of different
  kinds: [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).12 gives the first rung a live source, so
  "there are no priors" is a fact about `backends/observations/` rather than
  about the wiring; [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8 requires an applicable set
  whose projection output **differs** from `base_order`, so the two rungs are
  distinguishable by construction rather than by luck — the same discipline (7)
  imposes by requiring an inversion and not a shift; and
  [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s delivered-path clause asserts the rungs as two
  statements over three driven wakes. Any one alone leaves the mode open: the
  source alone is unexercised, the fixture alone is unasserted, and the guard
  alone was exactly what passed while the mode was live.
- **The variant and the slot are two axes, and conflating them is invisible to
  every mechanical check that does not read a trace.** A delivery that returns
  the `disabled` context when its store read comes back empty compiles, orders
  identically in the common case — Tier 1 is unavailable either way — and differs
  only in what it writes down: a claim that the operative policy declares no
  telemetry, in every decision the repository makes, while
  `policy/scheduler.yaml` declares some. Nothing fails until a reader tries to
  recompute the exclusion from the trace and finds no slot to recompute, or until
  someone edits the policy to actually disable telemetry and cannot tell the two
  states apart in the ledger. [W9](#el:w9-evidence-context).8 makes the crossing
  unconstructible in the assembly, [W14](#el:w14-driver-wiring).9 makes it a
  failure of the delivered path, and
  [W18](#el:w18-guard-dominance)/[W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades) assert the
  corrected states in traces the guards read.
- **The delivered-path guard clauses are the ones most likely to be quietly
  weakened into fixture clauses.** Every one of them is harder to write than the
  corpus clause beside it: it needs a materialized graph, the Driver's own entry
  point, and two runs to compare. The tempting weakening is to call the assembly
  directly and pass its result to `order`, which reproduces every asserted value
  and checks nothing about the wiring. The tell is textual and worth looking for
  in review: a delivered-path clause that never names the Driver's placement
  entry point is not one. [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus).8 states the data those
  clauses need precisely so the weakened form is visibly missing inputs rather
  than merely differently written, and `p16`–`p21` name the Driver placement
  files among their inputs for the same reason.
- **The trace and the fold must not disagree about `unsampled`.** The member
  name is inside the serialization the digest covers, so a rename is not a
  refactor: it makes the fold a different fold that cannot rebuild to the
  declared digest. [W6](#el:w6-snapshot-shape).7 states the rule at the shape,
  where it binds both producers.
- **The completion partition fails silently or not at all.** Every other rule in
  this plan announces its own violation — a policy refuses, a call is unlawful,
  a digest differs. Completion mass that never reached a rate announces nothing:
  the body is smaller, the roots still agree, the rebuild still matches, and the
  only visible effect is a merit number that is quietly too kind. That is why
  the equality is stated at the shape rather than only in the fold, checked by
  the screen independently of derivation agreement, and given fixtures on both
  sides of the boundary in two guards. Any one of those alone leaves the failure
  mode open; the shape rule alone would let a fold publish a lawful-looking
  smaller body, and the agreement check alone certifies a consistent omission.
- **The thresholds API is a dependency, not a coincidence.**
  [W10](#el:w10-comparison) compares statistics against margins that only
  [W3](#el:w3-policy-validator) produces, and the two packets sit on otherwise
  disjoint chains — one through the snapshot, one through the policy — so
  nothing else in the graph forces their order. That is exactly the shape
  RPL-001 caught, and it is the shape to look for elsewhere: a packet whose
  reads span two chains needs an edge on both.
- **Guard scope is large and the corpus is shared.** The five guard packets are
  mutually parallel and each owns its own fixture subtree, so they can be built
  concurrently; they all depend on [W15](#el:w15-fixture-corpus), which is the
  single ordering constraint among them.
- **The one way this encoding can fail to compile is a store import.**
  [W5](#el:w5-backend-context) and [W12](#el:w12-call-coherence) both assemble a
  declaration context "in force at a ledger position", which reads as an
  invitation to fetch it. Under [EN1](#el:en1-package-placement) the records
  arrive as an argument; the moment one of those files imports `internal/store`
  the build cycles, and `go build ./...` in every gate is what catches it
  immediately rather than three packets later.
- **`prefer_rank` must not move with a tuple statistic.** This is the one
  property most likely to be lost to a well-meaning implementation that "uses
  the best evidence available". [W11](#el:w11-ordering-seam).2 states it as the
  argument list, and [W20](#el:w20-guard-degrades)'s channel-separation fixture
  fixes an expected vector on both sides of the change.

## Work graph {#el:work-graph}

Twenty-two packets, one per work element, in the authored semantic application
order the `index` fixes. Every list field is explicit, including the empty ones.
The dependency edges satisfy both clauses of the produced-input closure rule
stated in [Work-graph dependency closure](#el:verification-closure): no packet
declares an input that an unordered sibling produces, and no packet reads
base-state a file whose staged form its own acceptance criteria cannot use.

Each `purpose` states what the packet builds and over what scope, and nothing
else. Under D0003.C4 the reasons, alternatives and causal explanations behind
these boundaries are carried by the L3 element each packet's `derived_from`
anchors — [W1](#el:w1-projection-record)–[W22](#el:w22-live-evidence-path) and
the [encoding decisions](#el:encoding) — and are not restated here. That rule is
[RPL-006](#el:revision)'s disposition and it binds this revision too: `p22`'s
`purpose`, the only new one, states the work and its scope and gives no reason,
and the clauses appended to seven other purposes are cases and conditions rather
than explanations.

Relative to the refused block, [RPL-008](#el:revision) adds **no packet and no
edge**. It extends `p14`'s and `p20`'s `inputs` with the observation reader the
live prior set comes from, and rewrites the `purpose` of `p14`, `p15`, `p18`,
`p20`, `p21` and `p22` where each stated the no-store context variant or the
cold-start floor. Every other field of every other packet is byte-for-byte
identical; every `id`, `derived_from`, `outputs`, `write_scope`,
`acceptance_checks` and `depends_on` value is unchanged everywhere; the authored
`index` is unchanged; and no packet was added, removed or reordered.
