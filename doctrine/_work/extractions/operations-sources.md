# Operations Sources: Evidence Extraction

Status: complete source-lane extraction for synthesis. This is not a book summary, and the source is not treated as normative by reputation alone. Case-study chapters supply consequence evidence for stated rules; they are cited as corroboration, not as independent rule sources.

## Scope, source IDs, and locator convention

The converted Markdown set for the assigned source was inspected in full, including front matter, part dividers, case studies, the bibliography, and the index. Reference-only and structural files appear in the coverage ledger but do not independently support actionable doctrine.

- `RI` — *Release It!: Design and Deploy Production-Ready Software, Second Edition* (Michael T. Nygard, 2018).
  - `RI_ROOT = books/release-it-design-and-deploy-production-ready-software-pdfdrive`

Every locator below and in the derived doctrine has the form `RI: chapters/<file> :: <Markdown heading>`. It is relative to the declared source root and resolves to an exact converted file and heading. All cited headings were verified to resolve exactly once per file and to carry `role: section` in `doctrine/section-maps/release-it-design-and-deploy-production-ready-software-pdfdrive.yaml`; conversion-flattened embedded headings (for example `Breaking API Changes`, `Designing the Experiment`, `Immutable and Disposable Infrastructure`) are never used as locators, and their content is attributed only to the enclosing citable section. Claims are paraphrases; numerical values, case-study particulars, and 2018-era product names remain contextual unless the repository supplies corroborating evidence.

## Per-theme evidence notes

### Stability physics and designed failure modes (chapters 2-5)

The stability lane is the book's core contribution. Chapter 3 supplies the fault -> error -> failure chain, the crumple-zone stance (failure modes are designed, or dangerous ones emerge on their own), and records the community split between fault-tolerant catch-and-contain and let-it-crash restart-from-known-state — preserved as `conflict-fault-tolerance-vs-let-it-crash` rather than resolved. Chapter 4 is a consequence-rich antipattern catalog (integration points, cascading failures, blocked threads, slow responses, force multiplication, unbounded result sets); chapter 5 is the corresponding costed countermeasure catalog, extracted as technique records (timeouts, circuit breaker, bulkheads, fail fast, let it crash, handshaking, test harness, decoupling middleware, shed load, back pressure, governor) with the source's own warning that pattern count is not a quality metric. Chapter 2's airline outage corroborates the failure-policy and blocked-thread claims with a real, costed incident.

### Production foundations, control plane, and security (chapters 6-11)

Chapters 7-8 ground instance-level doctrine: explicit network binding on multihomed hosts, ephemeral identity/clock/storage in virtualized environments, build chain of custody, externalized configuration, and transparency as a designed-in quality. Chapter 9 supplies the sharpest overload doctrine (every failing system starts with a queue backing up; shed load at the edge via health checks and residence-time-sized listen queues) plus dynamic service discovery as a costed technique. Chapter 10 gives automation-amplified failure evidence (AWS S3 2017, Reddit 2016) and concrete safeguards — rate limits on destructive change, minimum-capacity floors, human confirmation beyond large-change thresholds — which resonate with this library's authority-bounded-action doctrine. Chapter 11 contributes the untrusted-request, least-privilege, credential-custody, and dependency-vulnerability-currency records; all OWASP item details sit under embedded headings inside the section spans and are attributed only at section scope.

### Delivery, versioning, and adaptation (chapters 12-17)

Chapter 13 defines zero-downtime rollout as a designed time interval (prepare, drain, apply, warm-up) and deepens `refactoring-compatibility-migration` with expand/contract schema phasing, the undetectability criterion for constraints, and trickle-then-batch migration. Chapter 14 supplies the live-interface compatibility algebra (require less, accept more, return more; the live implementation is the de facto specification) and consumer-owned contract/conformance testing. Chapter 16 contributes Baldwin and Clark's modular operators (routed to `architecture-selective-option-preservation`), plural information models (routed to `domain-bounded-context`), and the efficiency-vs-adaptability warning. Chapter 17 anchors `operations-chaos-experiment-discipline` and `operations-resilience-margin` (drift into failure, regulator paradox, safety is not composable) and seeds `conflict-efficiency-vs-resilience-margin`. Case-study chapters 12 and 15 are cited only as corroborating consequence evidence.

### Deliberately not extracted

Org-process and infrastructure-procurement material below the engineering-agent doctrine threshold: "Development Is Production" (dev tooling deserves production SLAs), host-DNS availability placement, GSLB mechanics, hardware-vs-software load balancer selection, TIME_WAIT tuning, session-stickiness tradeoffs, and platform-team organizational design. `operations-feedback-paced-change` (chapter 16 decision-loop pacing) was considered and dropped as organization-level process doctrine; its thrashing evidence remains available at `RI: chapters/024-chapter-16-adaptation.md :: Process and Organization` should a conduct-lane pass later want it.

### Source limitations carried into the corpus map

Published 2018; cloud, container, tooling, and product specifics have aged. Evidence is predominantly first-person case studies — real failures with real costs, but anecdotal and survivorship-prone rather than controlled. Numerical details (thread counts, thresholds, dollar figures) are consequence illustrations, not portable gates. Single-source support caps every derived record at strong or contextual confidence.
