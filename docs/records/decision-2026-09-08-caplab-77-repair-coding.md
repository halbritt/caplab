## Observations and inference

The current CAPLAB-77 description proposes roughly ten archived review-dissent
or preference transcripts per harness, provisional transcript codes and
heterogeneous double-coding. It calls transfer to repair tasks an upper-bound
indication. A later amendment qualifies the transfer assumption and requires
capture configuration first, but retains the archived experiment. Its outcome
branches can confirm a harness recommendation, remove transcript codes, or
reduce harness selection to convenience.

[CAPLAB-79's capture decision](decision-2026-09-08-caplab-79-capture-design.md)
requires a new comparison on representative repair episodes under maximal
exposed capture. The prior episode population and capture configuration cannot
establish that requirement. This inspection reads the planning text, not the
archived episodes; it makes no new claim about their actual contents.

The implemented [agreement diagnostic](../product/contracts/code-agreement-report-v1.md)
reports complete-pair agreement by world/code and explicitly does not establish
accuracy, representativeness or blinding. Its chance-agreement term depends on
the two coders' marginal label frequencies. The
[primary definition](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.cohen_kappa_score.html)
supports that arithmetic; it does not supply a transfer bound across task
populations. Without a justified relationship between those populations,
calling archived agreement an upper bound is unsupported.

A new synthetic check exercised the unchanged CLI with two 100-pair joint
tables. The column order below is coder A then coder B; these are invented
labels, with no task truth or actual coders attached.

| Constructed case | 00 | 01 | 10 | 11 | Observed agreement | Chance agreement | Kappa |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Balanced margins | 40 | 10 | 10 | 40 | 4/5 | 1/2 | 3/5 |
| Skewed margins | 80 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 4/5 | 41/50 | -1/9 |

Both CLI calls exited 0 and matched separately evaluated exact rational
arithmetic. The same observed agreement yields different kappa values; assigning
either table to either task population is unconstrained by that arithmetic.
This illustrates why an assumed direction of transfer needs evidence. It does
not estimate a real transfer effect, coder accuracy or either harness's quality.

`/tmp/caplab-77-counterexample.json`, `/tmp/caplab-77-counterexample.log`, and

## Decision

Retarget CAPLAB-77 to **Measure coding reliability on representative repair
captures before harness selection**. Supersede its archived-task experiment as
a selection prerequisite and withdraw its unverified transfer bound and
automatic outcome branches. Preserve the original instruction as history.
No archived observation is invalidated, reclassified, rescored or discarded.

CAPLAB-77 owns the empirical coding comparison required by CAPLAB-63;
CAPLAB-84 supplies separately authorized repair captures after integration.
CAPLAB-79 supplies the selected capture requirements. This retains the
reliability question while eliminating a proxy experiment that cannot resolve
the current selection question. Leaving the old text operative invites the
wrong comparison. Cancelling the item entirely would leave coding reliability
without an empirical owner. Neither alternative is selected.

Before collection or coder exposure, freeze the comparison's exact native
Bindings, capture/redaction versions, development task population and sampling
plan, codebook, expected slots, coder assignment/order, missingness treatment,
uncertainty method and decision rules. Use comparable task inputs and a
prospective allocation across candidates; the resulting trajectories need not
be identical. Keep each harness/configuration, world and code distinct, and
include failure and unavailable-evidence cases under the frozen sampling rule.
Do not select only successful or easy-to-code transcripts. A difference between
whole native systems' action mixes cannot be attributed solely to log format.

Use the [code-authoring contract](../product/contracts/behavior-code-authoring-v1.md)
to establish observable meanings and positive/negative witnesses before coding.
These are development codes and episodes; they do not enter the selected study
through reuse or renaming. CAPLAB-65 must supply a coding roster and independently
grounded accuracy anchor; CAPLAB-66 must establish the coder-visible projection
and blinding feasibility before exposure. Reference labels stay out of coder
inputs. Preserve uncertainty about harness masking when native surface structure
reveals the candidate; treatment blinding and harness masking are separate.

Report each required world/code within each candidate configuration: expected,
complete, missing and unavailable pairs; the full joint table and marginal
counts; observed agreement and defined or undefined kappa; uncertainty under
the frozen method; and separately grounded accuracy/coverage evidence.
Retain codebook, coder and case-mix differences when interpreting comparisons.
A pooled statistic cannot conceal an uncodeable required world/code. The
existing diagnostic supplies descriptive counts and point estimates only;
it does not implement the required uncertainty or qualification gate.

Passing a reliability gate supports the specified coding use on that population,
subject to its separate accuracy and blinding requirements. It does not by
itself select a harness or establish reviewer capability. A failed or undefined
required gate withholds that use and invokes the frozen stop/amendment rule;
it does not prove all transcript-shaped codes impossible. Preserve the
[prospective failure disposition](decision-2026-09-08-code-agreement-diagnostic.md):
do not drop a failed required code after inspecting outcomes to clear the same
study. Any later artifact-only design requires a separate construct and
population decision, not a convenience fallback.

CAPLAB-63 retains harness selection across identity observability, write-set
recovery, missingness and the measured capture/coding constraints. Absence of
usable transcript codes does not establish equality on those dimensions.
CAPLAB-71 retains numerical parameters; no old threshold or sample count is
adopted here. CAPLAB-77 stays open until the prospective evidence and bounded
interpretation exist. This decision creates no execution budget.

Reopen this protocol if the selected capture design, study construct or target
task population changes, or if new evidence justifies a different transfer
claim. A cheaper archived investigation remains possible only under a separate
question and exact historical-evidence authorization.
